The Bible was written in real places by real people living through real historical events. This section connects Scripture to the broader sweep of ancient world history and to the archaeological discoveries that illuminate, confirm, and contextualize the biblical narrative.

## Archaeological Discoveries

Each note focuses on a specific artifact, inscription, or manuscript — physical evidence that illuminates or confirms the biblical narrative.

→ [[Archaeology — Map of Content|Browse all 9 archaeological discoveries →]]

| Artifact | Date | Held At | Biblical Connection |
|----------|------|---------|---------------------|
| [[Merneptah Stele]] | ~1208 BC | Egyptian Museum, Cairo | Earliest extra-biblical mention of Israel by name |
| [[Mesha Stele (Moabite Stone)]] | ~840 BC | Louvre, Paris | Names Omri; mentions YHWH; probable "House of David" reference |
| [[Tel Dan Stele]] | ~841–835 BC | Israel Museum, Jerusalem | First clear epigraphic evidence of the Davidic dynasty |
| [[Siloam Tunnel & Hezekiah's Inscription]] | ~701 BC | Istanbul Archaeological Museum | Hezekiah's water tunnel; one of the longest ancient Hebrew inscriptions |
| [[Sennacherib's Prism (Taylor Prism)]] | ~691 BC | British Museum, London | Siege of Judah; silence on Jerusalem's fall confirms 2 Kings 19 |
| [[Dead Sea Scrolls & Qumran]] | ~150 BC–68 AD | Israel Museum (Shrine of the Book) | Oldest biblical manuscripts; the Great Isaiah Scroll; the Essene community |
| [[Pilate Stone]] | ~26–37 AD | Israel Museum, Jerusalem | Only inscription naming Pontius Pilate; confirms his title "Prefect" |
| [[Ossuary of Caiaphas]] | 1st c. AD | Israel Museum, Jerusalem | Bone box of the high priest who presided at Jesus's trial |
| [[Gallio Inscription]] | ~52 AD | Delphi Archaeological Museum | Dates Paul's Corinthian ministry to 50–52 AD with precision |
