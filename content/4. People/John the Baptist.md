# John the Baptist

**Testament:** New Testament
**Era:** The Gospels
**Appears In:** [[3. Overviews/40. Matthew Overview|Matthew]], [[3. Overviews/41. Mark Overview|Mark]], [[3. Overviews/42. Luke Overview|Luke]], [[3. Overviews/43. John Overview|John]]

## Who He Is
John the Baptist is the last and greatest prophet of the old covenant — the Elijah-like forerunner sent to prepare Israel for the arrival of the Messiah. His entire life and ministry are defined by Isaiah 40:3: "A voice crying in the wilderness, 'Prepare the way of the Lord.'" Jesus himself declared, "Among those born of women there has arisen no one greater than John the Baptist" (Matthew 11:11) — yet John understood his own role perfectly: "He must increase, but I must decrease" (John 3:30).

## Why He Matters
- He stands at the hinge of the Testaments — the last in the long line of Israel's prophets and the first to point directly to Jesus as "the Lamb of God"
- His ministry of baptism for repentance prepared a people ready to receive the Messiah, and his call to "bear fruit in keeping with repentance" is as relevant as ever
- His posture of self-effacement before Jesus is a model for every preacher and disciple: the goal of ministry is to make Jesus larger, not oneself

## Character & Legacy
John was a man of the wilderness — clothed in camel's hair, eating locusts and wild honey, with no fixed home or institutional backing. His authority came entirely from God and his message was simple and unflinching: the ax is at the root of the trees, and religious heritage alone is no guarantee before a holy God. He was fearless before the powerful (he confronted Herod about his illegal marriage) and humble before Jesus (he protested his own unworthiness to baptize him).

His death marks the end of the old covenant prophetic era. Jesus's subsequent declaration of the Kingdom signals the dawn of the new. John is a transitional figure of the highest importance — the one who completed the race of OT prophecy and handed the baton directly to the Son of God.
