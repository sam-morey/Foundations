# Naomi

**Testament:** Old Testament
**Era:** The Judges
**Appears In:** [[3. Overviews/08. Ruth Overview|Ruth]]

## Who They Are
Naomi is an Israelite woman from Bethlehem who loses her husband and both sons while living as a foreigner in Moab. She returns home bereft and bitter, renaming herself "Mara" (bitter), only to find that God has not abandoned her. Her story traces the arc from desolation to restoration and serves as a picture of Israel in exile — emptied, grieving, yet carried home by a God who redeems.

## Why They Matter
- **Theological significance:** Naomi's willingness to voice her grief honestly — even her accusation that God has dealt bitterly with her — models a faith that does not suppress lament. God is not threatened by honest pain.
- **Role in redemptive history:** Naomi is the human thread that connects Ruth to Boaz and ultimately to David's line. Her wisdom in guiding Ruth, even in her brokenness, makes her an instrument of God's purposes she cannot fully see.
- **Legacy:** Naomi's story is the story of exile and return — emptied and brought back full. She is a type of Israel: scattered, suffering, yet not abandoned, and ultimately restored through the faithfulness of God and the loyalty of those around her.

## Character & Legacy
Naomi is a woman shaped by grief. She lost everything in a foreign land and returned home with nothing but a foreign daughter-in-law who refused to leave her. Her honesty about her pain is striking — she does not paste on a smile before God or her neighbors. She names her condition "bitter" and does not pretend otherwise. Yet underneath the grief is a woman of deep wisdom and care. She releases her daughters-in-law for their own good. She recognizes the hand of God in Boaz. She guides Ruth with shrewdness and maternal love.

The final scene of the book belongs to Naomi as much as to anyone: she holds the infant Obed in her arms while the neighbor women declare her life restored. The bitterness she named in chapter one is answered not with an explanation, but with a child — with life where there had been only death.

- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/58. Hebrews/Hebrews 11|Hebrews 11:13]] — Naomi lived as a stranger and exile, trusting in a homeland she could not yet fully see
