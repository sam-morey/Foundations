# Samuel

**Testament:** Old Testament
**Era:** The Judges
**Appears In:** [[3. Book Overviews/09. 1 Samuel Overview|1 Samuel]], [[3. Book Overviews/10. 2 Samuel Overview|2 Samuel]]

## Who They Are
Samuel is the last judge of Israel and the first of the great prophets, the man who stands at the transition from tribal confederation to monarchy. Dedicated to God before his birth by his mother Hannah, he hears God's voice as a child in the tabernacle, judges Israel faithfully his whole life, and anoints both of Israel's first two kings — Saul and David. He is the pivotal human figure between the age of judges and the age of kings.

## Why They Matter
- **Theological significance:** Samuel demonstrates that faithfulness to God's word — even when it is costly or unpopular — is the defining mark of a true prophet. His confrontation of Saul over incomplete obedience shows that partial obedience is disobedience.
- **Role in redemptive history:** Samuel anoints David, and it is David's line through which the Messiah will come. Samuel is therefore the human instrument through whom God sets the messianic lineage in place.
- **Legacy:** Samuel is remembered as one of the greatest intercessors in the Old Testament (Psalm 99:6; Jeremiah 15:1). His life models what it looks like to hear God's voice and obey it from childhood to death.

## Character & Legacy
Samuel's character is formed in the tabernacle. Sleeping near the ark of the covenant as a boy, he learns to recognize God's voice before he even knows whose voice it is. He grows up in the presence of Eli, witnessing both genuine faith and the devastating consequences of a priest who honored his sons above God. Samuel chooses differently — his life is one of consistent, costly integrity.

His grief at Israel's rejection of God's kingship (1 Samuel 8) is palpable, yet he obeys and anoints the king they demanded. His grief at Saul's failure is personal and profound — "And Samuel did not see Saul again until the day of his death, but Samuel grieved over Saul" (1 Samuel 15:35). Samuel is not a cold-blooded bureaucrat of God; he loves the people he serves and mourns their failures. Yet he never lets his grief override his obedience to God's word.
