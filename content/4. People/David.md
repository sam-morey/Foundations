## Who They Are
David is Israel's greatest king, the shepherd-boy from Bethlehem who slays Goliath, writes psalms, and receives the covenant promise that his throne will endure forever. Called "a man after God's own heart," he is the standard against which all subsequent kings are measured. His failures — adultery with Bathsheba, the murder of Uriah — are as well-known as his triumphs, yet his genuine repentance demonstrates what it means to return to God after catastrophic sin. Jesus is the Son of David, the fulfillment of the Davidic covenant.

## Why They Matter
- **Theological significance:** David demonstrates that the measure of a man before God is not moral perfection but the direction of his heart — its capacity for genuine repentance, worship, and trust. God does not honor him because he is sinless but because when he falls, he returns.
- **Role in redemptive history:** The Davidic Covenant (2 Samuel 7) is the backbone of all messianic expectation. Every subsequent prophet, every royal psalm, and the entire New Testament announcement that Jesus is "Son of David" depends on this promise.
- **Legacy:** David is the lens through which Israel understands both kingship and the coming Messiah. He is shepherd, warrior, poet, and king — and in each role, he anticipates the one who will fulfill all these offices perfectly.

## Character & Legacy
David's greatness lies in his wholeness before God. He is not cautious or distant with God — he dances before the ark, he weeps over his enemies, he cries out in agony in the psalms. His emotional and spiritual life is fully engaged. His failures are real and devastating: the abuse of power with Bathsheba, the calculated murder of a loyal soldier, the years of consequences that follow. He does not escape the fallout. But his response to Nathan's confrontation — immediate, unqualified confession — is the model of repentance.

The psalms are his lasting gift. In them, David has given the whole human family a vocabulary for speaking to God in every condition: celebration, anguish, doubt, trust, praise, lament. No other individual contributed more to how God's people pray.
