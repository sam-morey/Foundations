## Who They Are
Elisha is the successor of Elijah and one of the most prolific miracle-workers in the Old Testament. He asks for a double portion of Elijah's spirit, and the biblical narrative delivers exactly that — his miracles outnumber Elijah's. He raises the dead, heals leprosy, feeds a crowd with little, makes an iron ax head float, and the breath of his dead body raises another corpse. His ministry prefigures the works of Jesus with striking specificity.

## Why They Matter
- **Theological significance:** Elisha's ministry demonstrates that God's power to heal, restore, and reverse death is not confined to one chosen instrument — it flows through those who receive God's Spirit and walk in his ways. The miracles are signs that God is present and active among his people.
- **Role in redemptive history:** Elisha's miracles serve as a prophetic preview of the messianic age. His healing of a Gentile (Naaman), raising of the dead, and feeding of a crowd map directly onto Jesus's ministry, showing that God's saving intention always reached beyond Israel.
- **Legacy:** Elisha's ministry establishes the prophetic schools of Israel and provides a sustained witness to God's power during a period of deep spiritual decline in the Northern Kingdom. Even in death, his bones have more life in them than the living kings around him.

## Character & Legacy
Elisha's defining characteristic is accessibility. Unlike Elijah, who is often solitary and confrontational, Elisha is embedded in communities — he is known by the Shunammite woman, by the widow with the oil jar, by the poisoned stew, by the schools of prophets. He appears repeatedly in ordinary domestic settings and brings extraordinary divine power into them. He is a prophet of nearness.

His willingness to help the foreign Naaman — and his rebuke of Gehazi's greed in trying to profit from it — shows that Elisha understood the grace of God as boundless and not for sale. The healing of a Gentile military commander who then declares "there is no God in all the earth except in Israel" is a stunning foreshadowing of the Gentile mission. The prophet of the Northern Kingdom becomes an instrument of light to the nations.

- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/40. Matthew/Matthew 14|Matthew 14:13–21]] — Jesus feeding 5,000 echoes Elisha feeding 100 men with twenty loaves (2 Kings 4:42–44)
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/42. Luke/Luke 7|Luke 7:11–17]] — Jesus raising the widow's son at Nain echoes Elisha raising the Shunammite's son
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/40. Matthew/Matthew 8|Matthew 8:1–4]] — Jesus healing lepers fulfills and surpasses the pattern of healing Elisha inaugurated with Naaman
