## Who They Are
Sarah (originally Sarai) is the wife of Abraham, the mother of Isaac, and the ancestral mother of the Jewish people. She is a co-recipient of the covenant promise, and her ability to conceive in extreme old age is presented as a miracle rooted in the faithfulness of God. Her name is changed by God just as Abraham's is, signifying that she is a full participant in the covenant purpose, not merely a supporting figure.

## Why They Matter
- **Theological significance:** Sarah's barrenness and miraculous conception establish that the covenant line does not advance through human effort or natural ability — it advances through God's creative power. The son of promise is not produced by flesh but by faith.
- **Role in redemptive history:** Sarah is named in Hebrews 11 and praised in 1 Peter 3. Paul uses her and Hagar in Galatians 4 as an allegory of the two covenants — Sarah representing the Jerusalem above, the mother of all who are free in Christ.
- **Legacy:** Her laughter of disbelief became the name of her son — Isaac means "he laughs" — a permanent, joyful reminder that God gets the last laugh over human impossibility.

## Character & Legacy
Sarah is not merely a passive figure. She takes initiative — sometimes faithfully, sometimes not. Her suggestion to use Hagar as a surrogate reflects a common ancient Near Eastern practice, but it is also a failure of trust in God's timing. The conflict with Hagar and Ishmael that follows haunts the family and, in Paul's reading, the entire history of the two covenant communities.

Yet Sarah is also commended in 1 Peter 3 as a model of a woman who trusted God and honored her husband. Her faith, though imperfect, was genuine. When Isaac is born she does not merely celebrate a personal victory — she declares that God has made laughter for her and invites others to rejoice with her. Her story ends with Abraham mourning her death (Genesis 23) in the one act of recorded grief in his narrative, suggesting a depth of love and partnership that the text only briefly glimpses.
