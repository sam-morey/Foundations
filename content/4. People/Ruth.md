# Ruth

**Testament:** Old Testament
**Era:** The Judges
**Appears In:** [[3. Book Overviews/08. Ruth Overview|Ruth]], [[3. Book Overviews/40. Matthew Overview|Matthew]]

## Who They Are
Ruth is a Moabite widow who, after the death of her husband, chooses to remain with her mother-in-law Naomi and follow the God of Israel. She is the great-grandmother of David and an ancestor of Jesus Christ. Her story is the defining Old Testament portrait of hesed — covenant loyalty and loving-kindness — and stands as a remarkable example of a Gentile embraced by the God of Israel.

## Why They Matter
- **Theological significance:** Ruth embodies hesed — the faithful, loyal love that God himself shows his people. Her commitment to Naomi when every practical reason urged her to leave illustrates what covenant faithfulness looks like in human form.
- **Role in redemptive history:** As a Gentile woman who enters Israel's covenant community and becomes an ancestor of David and ultimately of Christ, Ruth foreshadows the Gentiles being grafted into God's people through faith.
- **Legacy:** Ruth is one of only five women named in Matthew's genealogy of Jesus, a signal that God builds his redemptive story through unexpected, outsider figures transformed by grace.

## Character & Legacy
Ruth's defining characteristic is loyal love that goes beyond obligation. She had every right to return to her own people and her own gods, yet she clings to Naomi in a declaration that is both personal and theological. She does not know Boaz exists when she makes this vow — she simply chooses God and Naomi regardless of cost.

In Bethlehem, Ruth displays industry, humility, and courage. She works hard in the fields and follows Naomi's guidance in approaching Boaz. She does not demand her rights but positions herself humbly. Her reward is not merely a husband — it is a place in the people of God and in the very lineage of the Messiah. A Moabite outcast becomes the great-grandmother of Israel's greatest king.

## New Testament Connections
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/40. Matthew/Matthew 1|Matthew 1:5]] — Ruth is named in the genealogy of Jesus, placing a Moabite woman in the direct line of the Messiah
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/58. Hebrews/Hebrews 11|Hebrews 11]] — Ruth's faith in leaving her homeland echoes the faith of Abraham; she is an unsung member of the faith hall of fame
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/45. Romans/Romans 4|Romans 4:16–17]] — Ruth's story illustrates that covenant membership is by faith, available to all nations, not by ethnic descent
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/42. Luke/Luke 1|Luke 1:38]] — Ruth's surrender to God's plan ("your God will be my God") prefigures Mary's "let it be to me according to your word"
