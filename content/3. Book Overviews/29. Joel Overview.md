## Summary

Joel begins with a devastating locust plague that strips the land bare — used as a picture of the coming "Day of the Lord." The prophet calls Judah to sincere repentance, promising that God will restore what the locusts have eaten. The book pivots dramatically in chapter 2 to an eschatological promise: God will pour out his Spirit on *all flesh* — sons and daughters, old and young, servant and free. Peter quotes this at Pentecost (Acts 2) as its fulfillment, making Joel one of the most directly cited OT texts in the NT.
