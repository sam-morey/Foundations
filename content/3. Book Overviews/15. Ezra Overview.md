# Ezra — Overview

**Author:** Ezra (traditional)
**Date:** ~5th century BC
**Genre:** Historical Narrative
**Key Themes:** [[Covenant]], [[Law & Torah]], [[Grace & Mercy]], [[Prayer & Worship]]

## Summary
Ezra records the return of Jewish exiles to Jerusalem under Zerubbabel (first wave) and Ezra (second wave). The Temple is rebuilt and the community is reconstituted around Torah faithfulness. Ezra the scribe becomes a model of devotion to the Word of God.

## Notable People
| Person | Role | Why They Matter |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| [[4. People/Ezra\|Ezra]] | Scribe and priest | His devotion to the Law and his public reading and teaching model the role of the Word in covenant renewal; Paul's description of Scripture's role in the church echoes Ezra's ministry |
| Zerubbabel | Governor and Temple rebuilder | Leads the first wave of returnees and oversees the rebuilding of the Temple's foundation; he is of Davidic descent, keeping the messianic line alive after exile |
| Cyrus | King of Persia | Though a pagan king, God stirs his spirit to issue the decree of return (ch. 1); Isaiah named him 150 years in advance as God's "shepherd" — one of Scripture's most striking predictive prophecies |
