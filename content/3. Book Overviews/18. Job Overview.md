# Job — Overview

**Author:** Unknown
**Date:** Unknown (events may be patriarchal; text could be much later)
**Genre:** Wisdom / Poetry / Drama
**Key Themes:** [[Suffering & Perseverance]], [[Wisdom]], [[Faith & Trust]], [[Judgment]]

## Summary
Job is the Bible's most extended meditation on suffering and theodicy. A righteous man loses everything; his friends offer inadequate theological explanations; Job cries out to God in honest anguish. God answers — not with an explanation, but with a revelation of himself. Job is restored, having seen God directly.

## Structure
| Section | Chapters | Content |
|---------|----------|---------|
| Prologue | 1–2 | Job's trials; the heavenly court; "Shall we receive good and not evil?" |
| Dialogue: Job & Friends | 3–31 | Three cycles of speeches; Job's raw lament and protest |
| Elihu | 32–37 | A younger voice intervenes |
| God speaks | 38–41 | God answers from the whirlwind; "Where were you?" |
| Epilogue | 42 | Job's restoration; his friends rebuked |
