# Mark — Overview

**Author:** John Mark (companion of Peter)
**Date:** ~50–60 AD
**Genre:** Gospel
**Key Themes:** [[Messiah & Christ]], [[Kingdom of God]], [[Sacrifice & Atonement]], [[Suffering & Perseverance]]

## Summary
Mark is the shortest and most urgent Gospel — full of action ("immediately," "at once," "straightway"). It presents Jesus as the powerful Son of God who serves and gives his life as a ransom. The "Messianic Secret" (Jesus repeatedly silences those who recognize him) runs throughout. The climax is the centurion's confession at the cross.

## Notable People
| Person | Role | Why They Matter |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| [[4. People/Jesus\|Jesus]] | The Son of God and Servant-King | Mark presents Jesus with urgent power — exorcising demons, healing the sick, calming storms — before revealing the cost: the Son of Man came to give his life as a ransom for many |
| [[4. People/John the Baptist\|John the Baptist]] | Forerunner of Jesus | He opens the narrative, fulfilling Malachi 3 and Isaiah 40; his imprisonment foreshadows the suffering that awaits Jesus |
| [[4. People/Peter\|Peter]] | Prominent disciple | Peter's confession at Caesarea Philippi (ch. 8) is the turning point of Mark; his failure at the denial and the angel's special word to him at the empty tomb frame his story with grace |
| [[4. People/Judas Iscariot\|Judas Iscariot]] | Betrayer of Jesus | His agreement to hand Jesus over for money sets the Passion in motion; Mark records the act without extended commentary, letting the weight speak for itself |
| Herod Antipas | Tetrarch of Galilee | He imprisons and beheads John the Baptist at the request of his wife Herodias — a chilling picture of political cowardice and moral corruption |
