# Esther — Overview

**Author:** Unknown
**Date:** ~5th–4th century BC
**Genre:** Historical Narrative
**Key Themes:** [[Grace & Mercy]], [[Judgment]], [[Israel & The Gentiles]]

## Summary
Esther tells how a Jewish woman becomes queen of Persia and, through her courage and the wisdom of her cousin Mordecai, saves the Jewish people from genocide. God is never named in the book, yet his providence is visible throughout. The events give rise to the festival of Purim.

## Notable People
| Person | Role | Why They Matter |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| [[4. People/Esther\|Esther]] | Jewish queen of Persia | Her courage in approaching the king unsummoned — "If I perish, I perish" — models bold faith in God's unseen providence; she is a model of using one's position for God's purposes |
| [[4. People/Mordecai\|Mordecai]] | Esther's cousin and guardian | His refusal to bow to Haman triggers the crisis; his counsel to Esther — "Who knows if you have come to the kingdom for such a time as this?" — is the book's theological center |
| Haman | Prime minister and antagonist | His genocidal plot against the Jews is foiled by the very scheme he devised; his fate illustrates the principle that those who seek to destroy God's people are destroyed themselves |
