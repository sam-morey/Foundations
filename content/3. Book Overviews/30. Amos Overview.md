# Amos — Overview

**Author:** Amos of Tekoa | **Date:** ~760–750 BC | **Genre:** Prophetic | **Key Themes:** [[Justice & Righteousness|Justice & Righteousness]] · [[Judgment|Judgment]] · [[Israel & The Gentiles|Israel & The Gentiles]] · [[Covenant|Covenant]]

---

## Summary

Amos is a shepherd and fig-grower from Tekoa (Judah) sent north to prophesy against Israel at the height of its prosperity under Jeroboam II. His indictment is searing: the wealthy are growing rich by crushing the poor, the courts are corrupt, and the worship feasts are hollow performance. The most famous verse in the book — "Let justice roll down like waters, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream" (5:24) — is one of the Bible's most quoted prophetic demands. Amos closes with a promise of restoration: the fallen booth of David will be rebuilt (9:11), quoted by James at the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15) to justify Gentile inclusion.

## Structure

| Section | Chapters | Content |
|---------|----------|---------|
| Oracles Against the Nations | 1:1–2:16 | Judgment on surrounding nations, then Israel and Judah |
| Three Sermons Against Israel | 3–6 | Covenant privilege brings greater accountability; social indictment |
| Five Visions | 7–9:10 | Locusts, fire, the plumb line, the basket of summer fruit, the altar |
| Restoration Promise | 9:11–15 | The booth of David rebuilt; abundance and return from exile |
