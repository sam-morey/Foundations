# Leviticus — Overview

**Author:** Moses (traditional)
**Date:** ~15th–13th century BC
**Genre:** Law / Priestly Code
**Key Themes:** [[Sacrifice & Atonement]], [[Law & Torah]], [[Covenant]], [[Justice & Righteousness]]

## Summary
Leviticus provides the instructions for Israel's worship and communal life as a holy people before a holy God. Its central theme is holiness: "You shall be holy, for I the Lord your God am holy" (19:2). It is quoted extensively in Hebrews as the backdrop for understanding Christ's priesthood.

## Structure
| Section | Chapters | Content |
|---------|----------|---------|
| The Sacrificial System | 1–7 | Five offerings: burnt, grain, peace, sin, guilt |
| Ordination of Priests | 8–10 | Aaron and sons consecrated; Nadab and Abihu |
| Clean and Unclean | 11–15 | Dietary laws, skin diseases, bodily discharges |
| Day of Atonement | 16 | *Yom Kippur*: the high point of Israel's year |
| Holiness Code | 17–27 | Ethics, festivals, Jubilee, covenant blessings/curses |
