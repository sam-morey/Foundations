# Romans — Overview

**Author:** Paul the Apostle
**Date:** ~57 AD (written from Corinth)
**Genre:** Epistle / Theological Treatise
**Key Themes:** [[Redemption & Salvation]], [[Faith & Trust]], [[Grace & Mercy]], [[Law & Torah]], [[Israel & The Gentiles]], [[Sin & The Fall]], [[Resurrection & Eternal Life]], [[The Holy Spirit]]

## Summary
Romans is Paul's most systematic theological letter — his fullest statement of the gospel. It demonstrates that all humanity stands condemned, that God's righteousness is revealed in the gospel through faith, and that this salvation has cosmic and historical implications (Israel and the Gentiles, creation's renewal). Chapters 12–16 apply this theologically dense gospel to everyday life.

## Structure
| Section | Chapters | Content |
|---------|----------|---------|
| Introduction | 1 | Paul, the gospel, and the theme: "the righteousness of God from faith for faith" |
| The Problem: Universal Condemnation | 1–3 | Gentiles, Jews — all under sin |
| The Solution: Justification by Faith | 3–5 | Righteousness through Christ; Abraham as model |
| New Life in Christ | 6–8 | Freedom from sin and death; life in the Spirit |
| The Jewish Question | 9–11 | God's faithfulness to Israel; the mystery of the Gentiles |
| Christian Ethics | 12–16 | Living sacrifice; love; government; the strong and weak |
