## Biblical Events
- ~1526 BC — Birth of Moses in Egypt; hidden in a basket; adopted by Pharaoh's daughter (Exod 2)
- ~1486 BC — Moses flees Egypt after killing an Egyptian; 40 years in Midian (Exod 2)
- ~1446 BC — The burning bush; God reveals the name YHWH; Moses commissioned (Exod 3–4)
- ~1446 BC — The ten plagues of Egypt; Passover institution; death of the firstborn (Exod 7–12)
- ~1446 BC — The Exodus from Egypt; crossing of the Red Sea (Yam Suph); Pharaoh's army destroyed (Exod 14)
- ~1446 BC — Arrival at Sinai; the Ten Commandments; the Mosaic covenant established (Exod 19–20)
- ~1446–1406 BC — Forty years of wilderness wandering following the report of the twelve spies (Num 13–14)
- ~1406 BC — Death of Moses on Mount Nebo; Israel poised to enter Canaan (Deut 34)

## World History Context

The New Kingdom of Egypt (c. 1550–1070 BC) was one of the most powerful and culturally sophisticated civilizations in the ancient world during the period of the Exodus. Pharaohs such as Thutmose III (r. 1479–1425 BC), often called the "Napoleon of the ancient world," campaigned extensively into Canaan and Syria, recording seventeen military campaigns and leaving detailed annals at Karnak. If the early date of the Exodus (~1446 BC) is correct, then the pharaoh of the oppression may have been Thutmose III and the pharaoh of the Exodus his son Amenhotep II (r. 1427–1400 BC). If a late date (~1270–1250 BC) is preferred by some scholars, Ramesses II (r. 1279–1213 BC) becomes the candidate — the builder of the store-cities of Pithom and Ramesses mentioned in Exodus 1:11.

The wider Near East during this period was dominated by a balance of great powers — Egypt in the south and west, the Hittite Empire in Anatolia, Mitanni in the north of Mesopotamia, and Babylon in the south. The Amarna Letters (c. 1360–1332 BC), a cache of 382 clay tablets found at Akhenaten's capital, reveal desperate appeals from Canaanite city-state rulers to the Egyptian court, including alarming references to groups called "Hapiru" (or Apiru) causing disruption — a term cognate with "Hebrew" that many scholars connect to the Israelite conquest period. The Hittite Empire, headquartered at Hattusa in modern Turkey, concluded the world's first recorded peace treaty with Egypt after the Battle of Kadesh (~1274 BC), a document that parallels the suzerainty-treaty form used in the Mosaic covenant at Sinai.

Ugaritic literature, discovered at Ras Shamra (ancient Ugarit, Syria) beginning in 1929, provides the closest cultural and linguistic parallel to Hebrew poetry and the Psalms. The Baal Cycle and other Ugaritic myths illuminate the religious landscape that Israel was called to reject — the worship of Baal, Asherah, and El — and help scholars understand the language and imagery of biblical poetry. In the Aegean, Mycenaean Greece was at its height, constructing the shaft graves and palace complexes at Mycenae and Tiryns that would later inspire Greek legend.

## Dominant Powers & Their Impact on Israel
| Power | Dates | Impact on Israel/Bible |
|-------|-------|------------------------|
| New Kingdom Egypt | 1550–1070 BC | Context for Israelite slavery, the Exodus, and Sinai; the defining formative event of Israel's national identity |
| Hittite Empire | 1650–1180 BC | Suzerainty-treaty form parallels the Mosaic covenant structure at Sinai (preamble, history, stipulations, curses/blessings) |
| Mitanni Kingdom | 1500–1300 BC | Northern Mesopotamian power; Hurrian cultural influence on Canaan during patriarchal/Mosaic period |
| Ugarit (city-state) | 1450–1185 BC | Ugaritic texts illuminate Canaanite religion and Hebrew poetic forms; contrast to monotheism of the Torah |
| Mycenaean Greece | 1600–1100 BC | Contemporaneous Aegean civilization; collapse ~1200 BC overlaps with Sea Peoples crisis (see Era 3) |

## Key Archaeological Evidence
- **Merneptah Stele** (c. 1208 BC, Egyptian Museum Cairo) — Victory inscription of Pharaoh Merneptah; contains the earliest extra-biblical mention of "Israel" as a people in Canaan: "Israel is laid waste, its seed is no more." Proves Israel existed as a distinct people by the late 13th century BC
- **Ipuwer Papyrus** (Leiden Museum; composition date debated, possibly 18th–17th c. BC) — Egyptian text describing societal chaos: rivers turning to blood, darkness, death of the firstborn-style calamities; some scholars see thematic parallels to the plagues of Exodus
- **Brooklyn Papyrus** (Brooklyn Museum, c. 1740 BC) — Administrative Egyptian document listing Hebrew/Semitic names of slaves, including names like Shiphrah (cf. Exod 1:15), confirming Semitic slaves in Egypt with biblical-sounding names
- **Deir el-Medina Ostraca** (Luxor, Egypt) — Records of the workers' village that built the royal tombs in the Valley of the Kings; detail the daily administrative life of Egyptian laborers, providing context for Israelite forced labor
- **Amarna Letters** (c. 1360–1332 BC, discovered 1887 at Tell el-Amarna) — 382 cuneiform tablets; Canaanite vassal correspondence to Pharaoh; references to "Hapiru" disrupting Canaan may overlap with Israelite settlement
- **Sinai Inscriptions at Serabit el-Khadim** (Sinai Peninsula) — Proto-Sinaitic script (~1500 BC), possibly the earliest alphabetic writing system, developed by Semitic turquoise miners in Egypt's employ — the linguistic world of Moses

## Notable Figures (Biblical & Historical)
| Figure | Role | Significance |
|--------|------|--------------|
| Moses | Prophet, lawgiver, mediator of the covenant | Greatest prophet of the OT (Deut 34:10–12); mediates the Sinai covenant; author of the Torah |
| Aaron | High priest; Moses' brother | First high priest; his line carries the Levitical priesthood through Israel's history |
| Miriam | Prophetess; sister of Moses | Leads worship after the Red Sea crossing (Exod 15); one of Israel's first named female leaders |
| Thutmose III | Pharaoh of Egypt | Leading candidate for pharaoh of the oppression (early date view); greatest military pharaoh of the New Kingdom |
| Amenhotep II | Pharaoh of Egypt | Leading candidate for pharaoh of the Exodus (early date view); his son's death in the tenth plague |
| Ramesses II | Pharaoh of Egypt | Builder of Pi-Ramesses; leading candidate for pharaoh of Exodus in late-date view; longest-reigning New Kingdom pharaoh |
| Akhenaten | Pharaoh; religious reformer | Briefly introduced monotheistic-style worship of Aten (~1353–1336 BC); some scholars speculate connection to Mosaic monotheism, though the biblical account points to a distinct divine revelation |
