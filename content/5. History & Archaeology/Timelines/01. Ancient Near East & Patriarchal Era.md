## Biblical Events
- ~2166 BC — Abraham born in Ur of the Chaldeans; called by God to leave for Canaan (Gen 12)
- ~2091 BC — Abraham enters Canaan; covenant of land and descendants established (Gen 12–15)
- ~2066 BC — Birth of Isaac; covenant confirmed through the line of promise (Gen 21)
- ~2006 BC — Birth of Jacob; later renamed Israel after wrestling with God at Peniel (Gen 32)
- ~1991 BC — Birth of Joseph; later sold into slavery by his brothers (Gen 37)
- ~1876 BC — Jacob's family (70 persons) descends to Egypt during famine; Joseph as vizier (Gen 46–47)
- ~1859 BC — Death of Jacob in Egypt; embalmed and buried at Machpelah (Gen 49–50)
- ~1805 BC — Death of Joseph in Egypt; the patriarchal era closes as Israel grows into a people (Gen 50)

## World History Context

The patriarchal age unfolded across a politically fragmented and culturally rich Near East. The Ur III dynasty of Sumer (c. 2112–2004 BC) represented the last great flowering of Sumerian civilization, with its elaborate bureaucracy, ziggurat temples, and hymns to the moon-god Nanna — the very religious world Abraham's family inhabited before the call of God (Josh 24:2). The Akkadian Empire of Sargon and Naram-Sin had already risen and collapsed, leaving behind a legacy of Semitic language spread and administrative models that shaped the entire region. When Abraham departed Ur, he was leaving one of the most sophisticated urban centers in the ancient world, a city of perhaps 65,000 inhabitants with a massive ziggurat complex at its heart.

The Middle Bronze Age (c. 2000–1550 BC) saw Canaan organized into a patchwork of city-states, each with its own king and patron deity — precisely the world Genesis depicts as Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob move between Shechem, Bethel, Hebron, and Beersheba. The Old Babylonian period produced Hammurabi (r. 1792–1750 BC), whose famous law code, inscribed on a black diorite stele now in the Louvre, provides the closest Near Eastern parallel to Mosaic covenant law in its structure of apodictic and casuistic legislation. Egypt's Middle Kingdom (c. 2055–1650 BC) maintained influence in Canaan through trade and military campaigns; the Story of Sinuhe from this period describes a Semitic fugitive thriving in Canaan, closely paralleling aspects of the patriarchal narratives.

To the east, the Indus Valley civilization (Harappa and Mohenjo-daro) was approaching its decline around 1900 BC, while in China the semi-legendary Xia dynasty was giving way to the Shang. These distant civilizations had no direct contact with biblical Canaan, but they underscore that the patriarchal period was a time of simultaneous urban emergence across the globe, a world in which God chose one family through whom to work his redemptive purposes.

## Dominant Powers & Their Impact on Israel
| Power | Dates | Impact on Israel/Bible |
|-------|-------|------------------------|
| Ur III (Sumer) | 2112–2004 BC | Birthplace of Abraham; polytheistic context from which God called his servant (Josh 24:2) |
| Akkadian Empire remnants | 2200–2000 BC | Spread Semitic language and trade networks across the Fertile Crescent |
| Old Babylonian Empire | 1894–1595 BC | Hammurabi's legal code parallels covenant-law forms; Amorite cultural milieu matches patriarchal names |
| Egypt Middle Kingdom | 2055–1650 BC | Joseph's rise to power as vizier; Israel's descent into Egypt begins the story of the Exodus |
| Hyksos (Egypt) | 1650–1550 BC | Semitic rulers of Egypt; may explain how a Hebrew could hold high office under a "pharaoh who knew Joseph" |

## Key Archaeological Evidence
- **Nuzi Tablets** (15th c. BC, Hurrian city southeast of Nineveh) — Describe customs strikingly similar to patriarchal practices: adopting a servant as heir (cf. Gen 15:2–3), a barren wife giving her handmaid to her husband (cf. Gen 16), and oral death-bed blessings having legal force (cf. Gen 27)
- **Mari Letters** (~1800–1750 BC, Tell Hariri, Syria) — Archive of 20,000+ cuneiform tablets from the palace of Zimri-Lim; contain Amorite personal names structurally identical to Hebrew patriarchal names (Abamram = Abraham-form; Aqba-Hammu; Banu-Yamina = "sons of the right hand" = Benjaminites)
- **Ebla Tablets** (~2400–2300 BC, Tell Mardikh, Syria) — Archive of 17,000 tablets; mention cities such as Sodom and Gomorrah in a sequence matching Genesis 14, and personal names paralleling biblical patriarchal names
- **Code of Hammurabi** (c. 1754 BC, Louvre Museum) — Black diorite stele with 282 laws; its "if…then" casuistic structure parallels Mosaic law forms; demonstrates that ancient covenant-law was a known genre
- **Execration Texts** (Middle Kingdom Egypt, c. 1900 BC) — Egyptian lists of enemy place-names in Canaan include cities also mentioned in the patriarchal narratives, confirming the inhabited landscape of the era
- **Tomb of Khnumhotep II** at Beni Hasan (c. 1890 BC) — Wall painting depicts a group of 37 Asiatic (Semitic) traders entering Egypt, offering a visual parallel to the kind of movement Abraham and Jacob's family made between Canaan and Egypt

## Notable Figures (Biblical & Historical)
| Figure | Role | Significance |
|--------|------|--------------|
| Abraham | Patriarch; father of the covenant people | Called from Ur; faith credited as righteousness (Gen 15:6); ancestor of Israel and Ishmael |
| Isaac | Second patriarch | Child of promise born to elderly parents; near-sacrifice at Moriah foreshadows Christ |
| Jacob / Israel | Third patriarch | Twelve sons become the twelve tribes; name "Israel" defines the nation |
| Joseph | Son of Jacob; Egyptian vizier | Type of Christ: betrayed, imprisoned, exalted, saves his brothers; bridges Genesis to Exodus |
| Hammurabi | King of Babylon | Codified Mesopotamian law; contemporary of the patriarchal period; provides legal-cultural context |
| Thutmose I / Senusret III | Egyptian pharaohs | Middle Kingdom rulers during Israel's early time in Egypt; context for growth of Israelite population |
