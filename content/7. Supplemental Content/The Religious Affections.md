## Synopsis
- Jonathan Edwards argues that true religion consists essentially in holy affections — not cold knowledge or mere external behavior, but the whole heart genuinely moved toward God.
- He gives twelve signs that do NOT reliably distinguish true from false religious experience, deflating both enthusiast and formalist criteria.
- He then gives twelve signs that DO reliably distinguish true religion, culminating in the test of holy practice — a transformed, Christlike life sustained over time.

## Source Info
- Title: A Treatise Concerning Religious Affections
- Author: Jonathan Edwards
- Publication Date: 1746
- Themes: Faith & Trust, Prayer & Worship, The Holy Spirit, Sin & The Fall, Wisdom

## Key Ideas
- The Great Awakening produced both genuine revival and counterfeit enthusiasm, and Edwards wrote this treatise to provide a diagnostic that could distinguish the two.
- Neither emotional intensity nor its absence is a reliable sign of true religion; the Holy Spirit works through the affections but does not validate every experience of strong feeling.
- The most reliable sign of genuine grace is its practical fruit — not what a person feels or says in the moment of religious experience, but how they live over time.
- Spiritual pride is the most dangerous counterfeit, because it mimics every virtue while being fueled by self-love rather than God-love.

## Chapter Summaries
- **Part 1 — The Nature and Importance of the Affections**
  - **Main Idea:** Edwards establishes that religion, if it is real, must engage the affections — not merely the intellect — because the affections are the seat of the will and the engine of all human action.
  - **Key Points:**
    - "True religion, in great part, consists in holy affections" — Edwards is not saying that religion is only feelings, but that it cannot exist without feelings properly engaged.
    - The affections include love, desire, hope, joy, gratitude, compassion, zeal, and their opposites — they are the inclinations that determine what a person actually pursues.
    - A person who knows Christian doctrine without any movement of the affections does not truly know it in the biblical sense — knowledge that does not move the will has not yet become real.
    - Scripture speaks constantly of holy affections: "love the Lord your God with all your heart," "delight yourself in the Lord," "hope in God," "rejoice in the Lord always."
  - **Defined Terms:**
    - **Affections:** For Edwards, not mere passing emotions but the vigorous, active inclinations of the soul — what you love, desire, fear, and pursue. They are the springs of action.
    - **Inclination:** The bent of the will toward or away from an object; the affections are the strong forms of inclination that visibly determine behavior.
  - **Takeaway:** A Christianity of the head alone is not the Christianity of Scripture — God designed human beings as affective creatures, and he demands the engagement of the whole person.

- **Part 2 — Twelve Signs That Are No Evidence of Genuine Grace (Negative Signs)**
  - **Main Idea:** Edwards systematically disqualifies twelve commonly used criteria for judging spiritual experience, demonstrating that none of them, taken alone or together, can reliably identify true grace.

  - **Negative Sign 1 — Intensity of Feeling**
    - **Main Idea:** The mere strength or intensity of a religious experience is no proof of its genuineness — counterfeit grace can be as intense as true grace, and sometimes more so.
    - **Key Points:**
      - The history of revivals shows that the most extravagant emotionalism is sometimes entirely natural or demonic in origin.
      - Genuine grace can be quiet and steady while false experience is loud and dramatic.
      - Intensity of feeling is evidence only that something is happening, not of what or of its source.
      - This sign neither confirms nor denies genuine grace — it is simply not evidence either way.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Enthusiasm:** The term Edwards uses for the extreme emotionalism of some revivals — treating intense subjective feeling as direct proof of the Spirit's work.
    - **Takeaway:** Do not judge spiritual experience by its emotional force; the question is always whether the object of the feeling is truly God.

  - **Negative Sign 2 — Physical Effects**
    - **Main Idea:** Bodily effects — trembling, fainting, crying out, visions — are no reliable indication of the Spirit's work, since all of these can arise from natural causes or demonic influence.
    - **Key Points:**
      - The body will respond to any very strong emotion, whether the source is divine or not — the physical response is caused by the mental state, not directly by the Spirit.
      - Scripture does record physical effects of divine encounters (e.g., Daniel's trembling), but it does not present them as criteria for validation.
      - Some of the most carnally motivated experiences in revival history were accompanied by the most dramatic physical effects.
      - Edwards is not dismissing physical effects but refusing to treat them as evidence.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Somatic Effects:** Bodily manifestations of intense mental or emotional states; Edwards accepts that they occur but denies that they validate the experience.
    - **Takeaway:** Physical manifestations of religious experience neither confirm nor deny the work of the Spirit — they tell you that a person is affected deeply, not whether the cause is genuine.

  - **Negative Sign 3 — Fluency About Religion**
    - **Main Idea:** The fact that a person talks a great deal about religion, quotes Scripture readily, and speaks enthusiastically about their experience is no evidence that the Spirit is at work.
    - **Key Points:**
      - Counterfeit experience produces a characteristic talkativeness — the person who is full of themselves will speak about themselves, even in religious language.
      - Scripture warns that many will say "Lord, Lord" on the last day who were never known by him (Matthew 7:22).
      - Genuine grace often produces more silence and self-distrust than the counterfeit, at least initially.
      - The love of talking about one's own spiritual state can be a form of spiritual pride.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Spiritual Talkativeness:** Edwards's implied category — the tendency of the self-focused religious person to speak constantly of their experiences as a form of self-display.
    - **Takeaway:** Eloquence about religion is not religion; the question is whether Christ or the self is at the center of the talk.

  - **Negative Sign 4 — Experiences Not Produced by the Self**
    - **Main Idea:** The fact that a religious experience was not self-produced — that it came suddenly and unexpectedly — does not prove it was divine.
    - **Key Points:**
      - Edwards's opponents argued that since Satan cannot produce genuine religious feelings, any spontaneous gracious experience must be from God.
      - But Satan can produce counterfeits that are not consciously self-induced — the human unconscious is capable of many things that feel externally given.
      - The source of an experience cannot be determined by asking whether the person tried to produce it.
      - The test must be applied to the content and fruit of the experience, not its mode of arrival.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Immediate Impression:** An experience that arrives without obvious human effort or mediation; Edwards denies that this is a mark of divine origin.
    - **Takeaway:** Whether you manufactured an experience or received it passively tells you nothing about whether it is from God.

  - **Negative Sign 5 — Texts of Scripture Coming to Mind**
    - **Main Idea:** The fact that a Scripture passage came suddenly and vividly to a person's mind during an experience does not prove the experience is from the Holy Spirit.
    - **Key Points:**
      - The practice of applying Scripture promises directly to oneself ("this verse is for me") can be a form of self-deception — Satan used Scripture when tempting Jesus.
      - The Spirit's work is attested by what the Scripture produces in the life, not by the manner of its arrival.
      - Many reported conversions in the Awakening involved dramatic impressions of Scripture that later proved empty.
      - The question is not which verses came to mind but whether they produced genuine spiritual fruit.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Impressed Texts:** Scripture passages that seem to come to the mind with special force or applicability; Edwards does not deny the Spirit can work this way but refuses to treat it as proof.
    - **Takeaway:** The Holy Spirit works through Scripture, but not every forceful impression of Scripture is the Holy Spirit — content and fruit matter more than manner.

  - **Negative Sign 6 — Apparent Love**
    - **Main Idea:** Apparent love — warmth, affection, and goodwill toward others in religious settings — is not in itself evidence of true grace because it can arise from a variety of non-gracious causes.
    - **Key Points:**
      - Natural warmth, social bonding, and emotional contagion in group settings can all produce feelings indistinguishable from Christian love.
      - The elder brother had been loyal for years — his behavior looked like love until the crisis revealed its true motivation.
      - True love is characterized not by warmth in settings that favor it but by commitment when it costs something.
      - Edwards does not deny that genuine grace produces love — he is denying that apparent love proves genuine grace.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Natural Affection:** Warmth and goodwill that arises from temperament, social context, or emotional contagion rather than from the renewing work of the Spirit.
    - **Takeaway:** Apparent love must be tested by what it does when it is inconvenient, costly, or unreciprocated.

  - **Negative Sign 7 — Multiple Experiences Together**
    - **Main Idea:** The combination of multiple religious experiences (joy, love, peace, light, zeal, Scripture impressions together) is still not conclusive evidence of genuine grace — the counterfeits often come in clusters.
    - **Key Points:**
      - If no single sign is reliable, the accumulation of unreliable signs does not become reliable.
      - Counterfeit grace tends to be comprehensive in its imitation — it replicates as many marks as possible.
      - Edwards is not saying multiple experiences are meaningless but that the reliable signs are qualitative, not cumulative.
      - The question is always about the nature and direction of the experiences, not their number.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Comprehensive Counterfeit:** Edwards's implicit concept — that false religion is often not one isolated error but a whole cluster of imitations that mirrors genuine religion closely.
    - **Takeaway:** More experiences of the same unreliable kind do not add up to evidence — you need a different quality of test, not more of the same test.

  - **Negative Sign 8 — Order of Experiences**
    - **Main Idea:** The fact that a person's religious experience followed a conventional pattern (conviction, terror, then light and peace) does not prove it was genuine — the pattern can be imitated.
    - **Key Points:**
      - Revivalist preaching created cultural expectations about how conversion should feel, and these expectations could shape experience without the Spirit's involvement.
      - A person who has heard many conversion narratives can unconsciously model their own experience on them.
      - Genuine grace is not tied to any particular emotional sequence — different people are converted in different ways.
      - The diagnostic is not the pattern but the fruit.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Conventional Conversion Narrative:** The expected sequence of conviction-terror-relief common in eighteenth-century New England revivalism; Edwards warns that cultural familiarity with this pattern can produce imitations.
    - **Takeaway:** Religious experience that conforms to the expected pattern may be culturally shaped rather than spiritually produced — the pattern is not the proof.

  - **Negative Sign 9 — Great Esteem for Others**
    - **Main Idea:** The fact that others — including godly ministers — speak highly of a person's religious experience is not a reliable sign that the experience is genuine.
    - **Key Points:**
      - Others are in no better position than the person themselves to apply the negative signs — they can only observe externals.
      - The Great Awakening produced many cases in which respected figures misjudged those who later proved unregenerate.
      - The approval of the church or of spiritual leaders is valuable but not authoritative on this question.
      - Edwards is not dismissing community discernment but qualifying it — it is subject to all the same limitations.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Community Discernment:** The judgment of the gathered church or of pastoral leadership about the spiritual state of individuals; real and useful but not infallible.
    - **Takeaway:** Even the best human judges of spiritual experience can be deceived — the reliable signs must be applied by each person to themselves.

  - **Negative Sign 10 — Confident Self-Assertion of Genuineness**
    - **Main Idea:** A person's own confident assertion that their experience is genuine — their certainty that they are saved — is not itself evidence that they are.
    - **Key Points:**
      - Hypocrites can be as confident of their salvation as true believers — in some cases more confident, since they have not yet encountered the tests that genuine faith passes.
      - "Lord, Lord" (Matthew 7:22) is spoken confidently by those who prove to have been workers of lawlessness.
      - Self-deception is not a surface problem easily penetrated by introspection — the heart is deceitful above all things (Jeremiah 17:9).
      - The reliable tests must be applied to one's life, not to one's confidence about one's life.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Presumptuous Assurance:** Confidence of salvation that is not grounded in genuine spiritual fruit but in the memory of an experience or the persuasion of others.
    - **Takeaway:** Confidence about one's spiritual state is not the same as being in a good spiritual state — the two can diverge dramatically.

  - **Negative Sign 11 — Visible Satisfaction of Others with One's Experience**
    - **Main Idea:** The visible satisfaction that others seem to take in the religion of a person is not evidence of that person's genuine spiritual state.
    - **Key Points:**
      - Others may be impressed by what is actually performance or by what is culturally competent religious behavior rather than genuine grace.
      - The Pharisees were publicly admired for their religious performance (Matthew 23:5–7) while being described by Jesus as whitewashed tombs.
      - Visible religion can be extremely convincing without being real.
      - This sign reinforces the general principle: all external and social criteria are insufficient.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Visible Religion:** The outward, observable dimension of religious life — attendance, speech, behavior, and affect in public settings; real but insufficient as evidence of genuine grace.
    - **Takeaway:** Religious performance that satisfies observers may be exactly that — performance — and the audience's satisfaction is not proof of its reality.

  - **Negative Sign 12 — External Manifestations of Grace in Conduct**
    - **Main Idea:** Even apparently holy conduct — prayer, reading Scripture, church attendance, charitable giving — is not in itself a reliable sign of genuine grace, because all of these can be performed for wrong reasons.
    - **Key Points:**
      - The Pharisees prayed, fasted, and gave alms conspicuously — and Jesus says they already have their reward (Matthew 6).
      - The person who is trying to earn salvation or preserve a reputation will produce the same external behaviors as the person who is responding to grace.
      - The question is not what a person does but why they do it — the direction and source of the will, not the form of the behavior.
      - Even this — the most apparently reliable external sign — falls short because it does not reach the heart.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **External Holiness:** Conduct that conforms to the shape of a holy life without necessarily arising from holy motivations; a form without the corresponding reality.
    - **Takeaway:** Religious behavior without the corresponding affections is the very thing Jesus condemned most sharply in the Pharisees — and it can deceive the person performing it as much as the people observing it.

- **Part 3 — Twelve Signs That Are Reliable Evidence of Genuine Grace (Positive Signs)**
  - **Main Idea:** Having cleared away twelve unreliable signs, Edwards proposes twelve positive signs that, taken together, reliably distinguish genuine grace — all of them concern the nature, direction, and quality of the affections and their fruit.

  - **Positive Sign 1 — They Are Spiritual in Origin**
    - **Main Idea:** Genuine holy affections arise from the supernatural work of the Holy Spirit, who communicates a new kind of perception — a spiritual sense — that was not present before regeneration.
    - **Key Points:**
      - The unregenerate person can have religious feelings, but they arise from natural faculties encountering religious objects.
      - The regenerate person has a new principle of spiritual perception — an "inward sweetness" for divine things that is qualitatively different from natural appreciation.
      - This new sense is not a sixth sense but a heightening and reorientation of the existing faculties by the Spirit.
      - 1 Corinthians 2:14 — "the natural person does not accept the things of the Spirit of God, for they are folly to him" — describes the pre-regenerate condition.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Spiritual Sense:** Edwards's key concept — the new faculty of perception given in regeneration by which the soul perceives the beauty and excellency of divine things; analogous to physical senses but concerned with spiritual realities.
    - **Takeaway:** Genuine grace involves a real change in what the person is able to perceive and love — not just a change in behavior but a change in seeing.

  - **Positive Sign 2 — Their Primary Object Is the Excellence of Divine Things**
    - **Main Idea:** Genuine holy affections are primarily attracted to the beauty and excellence of God himself, not merely to the benefits God provides (forgiveness, heaven, safety).
    - **Key Points:**
      - The unregenerate person may love God instrumentally — for what God gives — but not for who God is.
      - The regenerate person finds God himself beautiful — his holiness, his justice, his love, his majesty — and this is the primary draw.
      - The love of God's benefits without love of God himself is not genuine love of God but self-love wearing religious clothing.
      - Edwards cites Psalm 73:25 — "Whom have I in heaven but you? And there is nothing on earth that I desire besides you" — as the authentic voice.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Self-Love in Religion:** The motive of seeking God for personal benefit rather than for his own sake; Edwards identifies this as the defining motivation of counterfeit religion.
    - **Takeaway:** The first diagnostic of genuine grace is whether God himself — not merely what God provides — is the primary object of one's love and delight.

  - **Positive Sign 3 — They Arise from Moral Excellency**
    - **Main Idea:** True holy affections are rooted in a perception of the moral beauty of God and divine things, not merely in the awe-inspiring power or vastness of the divine.
    - **Key Points:**
      - Unbelievers can be awed by the power of God (storms, judgment, vastness) without being attracted to his holiness.
      - The regenerate person is moved specifically by the moral excellence of God — his love, his justice, his faithfulness, his grace.
      - This is the difference between fear of God as a powerful judge and love of God as a holy and gracious being.
      - Beauty-perception is the language Edwards uses — the converted person finds holiness beautiful where the unconverted found it threatening or dull.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Moral Beauty:** The excellence of God's character considered as an object of love and delight rather than merely as an object of power or fear.
    - **Takeaway:** True conversion changes what a person finds beautiful — holiness, which was previously unattractive or irrelevant, becomes the most compelling thing in existence.

  - **Positive Sign 4 — They Arise from the Enlightening of the Mind**
    - **Main Idea:** Genuine holy affections are always accompanied by genuine spiritual understanding — the heart and the mind are transformed together, and feeling without understanding is always suspicious.
    - **Key Points:**
      - The affections do not operate independently of the understanding; they are moved by what the understanding perceives.
      - The person who has strong feelings without any corresponding growth in knowledge of God and Scripture is in a dangerous condition.
      - 2 Peter 1:5–8 links virtue, knowledge, self-control, steadfastness, godliness, brotherly affection, and love — they grow together.
      - Enthusiast religion that disparages theological knowledge misunderstands the nature of genuine religious affections.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Spiritual Understanding:** The illumination of the mind by the Spirit to perceive the truth and beauty of divine things; Edwards insists it must accompany genuine affections.
    - **Takeaway:** Warm feelings without growing understanding are a warning sign; genuine grace renews the mind (Romans 12:2) as well as the affections.

  - **Positive Sign 5 — They Come with Spiritual Conviction of Reality**
    - **Main Idea:** Genuine holy affections bring with them a deep, settled conviction of the truth and reality of the things of religion — not a brittle certainty that crumbles under pressure but a durable persuasion.
    - **Key Points:**
      - The counterfeit often produces intense but unstable conviction — very certain at the moment of experience, but unable to maintain that certainty over time and against objections.
      - Genuine faith, for Edwards, involves a spiritual perception of the reality of unseen things that provides its own ground of assurance.
      - Hebrews 11:1 — "faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen" — describes this quality of perception.
      - The conviction is not primarily argumentative (though it survives argument) but experiential and perceptual.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Spiritual Conviction:** Not intellectual certainty alone but a deep, experiential persuasion that the realities of Scripture are truly real — a knowing that goes beyond inference to something like direct perception.
    - **Takeaway:** Genuine faith produces a settled persuasion of divine realities that does not depend on emotional intensity and does not evaporate when the emotion fades.

  - **Positive Sign 6 — They Come with Evangelical Humility**
    - **Main Idea:** Genuine holy affections always produce a growing humility — a deep and settled sense of one's own unworthiness before God — whereas counterfeit grace often produces pride.
    - **Key Points:**
      - Spiritual pride is Edwards's great diagnostic: the person who is growing in grace is growing in self-abasement; the counterfeit grows in self-exaltation.
      - The closer a person comes to God, the more clearly they see their own sinfulness — as Isaiah's vision (Isaiah 6) and Job's response (Job 40:4) illustrate.
      - Evangelical humility is different from the false humility that says "I am nothing" while actually seeking praise for the statement.
      - The holy person does not think well of themselves in comparison to others; they are too busy being overwhelmed by the holiness of God.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Evangelical Humility:** Humility that arises from a genuine spiritual sense of one's own sinfulness in the light of God's holiness; distinguished from temperamental meekness or social deference.
      - **Spiritual Pride:** The most dangerous counterfeit — the pride that disguises itself as spiritual advancement, comparing oneself favorably to others on spiritual grounds.
    - **Takeaway:** Watch for the direction of self-assessment over time — genuine grace produces growing humility, not growing spiritual confidence about one's own status.

  - **Positive Sign 7 — They Come with a Change of Nature**
    - **Main Idea:** Genuine holy affections are not periodic visitations but evidence of a real, underlying change in the nature of the person — a new disposition that is present even when the affections are not currently intense.
    - **Key Points:**
      - Regeneration is not an event that leaves the person unchanged but a transformation of the underlying inclinations of the soul.
      - The change of nature means that the person now has a habitual orientation toward God and holiness that was not present before.
      - This does not mean the person always feels holy or always has intense affections — but it means that when the soul is examined in depth, a new principle is present.
      - 2 Corinthians 5:17 — "if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation" — describes this ontological change.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **New Nature:** The regenerate principle implanted in the soul by the Spirit; not a replacement of the old nature but a new governing disposition that progressively gains influence over the whole person.
    - **Takeaway:** True grace is a change in what you are, not merely a change in what you feel — and this change should be increasingly visible over time.

  - **Positive Sign 8 — They Promote Christlike Meekness**
    - **Main Idea:** Genuine holy affections produce the spirit of Jesus — lamb-like, meek, forgiving, patient, and peaceable — whereas counterfeit grace tends to produce contentiousness, harshness, and spiritual aggression.
    - **Key Points:**
      - "Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart" (Matthew 11:29) — the pattern of genuine discipleship is the meekness of Christ.
      - Counterfeit revival religion often made people more proud and contentious, not less — Edwards witnessed this in the Awakening.
      - Meekness is not weakness or lack of conviction but the refusal to assert oneself or harm others in defense of one's own reputation.
      - The fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23) is the standard: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Christlike Meekness:** The disposition to respond to opposition, insult, and injury with patience and forgiveness rather than retaliation; modeled on the lamb-like character of Jesus.
    - **Takeaway:** Watch not for spiritual intensity but for increasing meekness — the person growing in grace is becoming more like the gentle and lowly Christ, not more combative.

  - **Positive Sign 9 — They Promote Tenderness of Spirit**
    - **Main Idea:** Genuine holy affections produce a heart that is tender — sensitive to sin, quick to feel sorrow over failures, easily moved with compassion for others.
    - **Key Points:**
      - A hardened heart is the sign of sin's progress; a softened heart is the sign of grace's progress.
      - The truly gracious person is not less sensitive to the sins of others after coming to faith but more — because they see sin more clearly in the light of God's holiness.
      - They are also more tender toward the suffering of others, more easily moved to compassion and intercession.
      - Ezekiel 36:26 — "I will remove the heart of stone from your flesh and give you a heart of flesh" — describes the transformation Edwards is pointing to.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Tenderness of Heart:** Spiritual sensitiveness — a heart that is easily affected by divine things, by the suffering of others, and by one's own sin; the opposite of hardness or callousness.
    - **Takeaway:** Growing in grace produces a growing capacity to feel — to be moved by beauty, grief, sin, and mercy — not a growing detachment or self-possession.

  - **Positive Sign 10 — They Produce Balance Among the Graces**
    - **Main Idea:** Genuine grace produces not one isolated virtue in excess but a balanced, symmetrical growth across all the Christian graces, whereas counterfeit grace tends to be lopsided.
    - **Key Points:**
      - A person who has zeal without love, or humility without courage, or joy without sorrow over sin, exhibits the profile of the counterfeit.
      - The saints of Scripture demonstrate a comprehensive holiness — they are loving and discerning, meek and courageous, joyful and penitent.
      - 2 Peter 1:5–7 lists the graces in sequence and implies they grow together: faith, virtue, knowledge, self-control, steadfastness, godliness, brotherly affection, love.
      - The absence of balance is a warning sign — obsession with any one virtue at the expense of others is characteristic of enthusiasm rather than genuine grace.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Symmetrical Grace:** Edwards's implied standard — holiness that is comprehensive, balanced, and integrated rather than exhibiting one virtue to excess while neglecting others.
    - **Takeaway:** Be suspicious of the religion that is all zeal and no gentleness, all joy and no repentance, all love and no truth — genuine grace is comprehensive.

  - **Positive Sign 11 — They Produce Growing Desire for God**
    - **Main Idea:** Genuine holy affections produce a hunger for more of God rather than a satisfied complacency — the person who has tasted grace desires more, whereas the counterfeit tends toward self-satisfaction.
    - **Key Points:**
      - Psalm 42:1 — "As a deer pants for flowing streams, so pants my soul for you, O God" — describes the quality of desire that genuine grace produces.
      - The regenerate person is never satisfied with their current state of knowledge of and communion with God — they are always hungering and thirsting for righteousness (Matthew 5:6).
      - Self-satisfaction is the mark of the counterfeit: "I am rich, I have prospered, and I need nothing" (Revelation 3:17).
      - The desire produced by genuine grace is oriented toward God himself, not toward spiritual experiences or feelings about God.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Spiritual Hunger:** The ongoing desire for God and for growth in grace that genuine conversion produces; an indicator of life rather than completion.
    - **Takeaway:** The person who believes they have arrived spiritually and no longer needs to grow is in a more dangerous condition than the person who is acutely aware of how far they have to go.

  - **Positive Sign 12 — They Produce Holy Practice**
    - **Main Idea:** The supreme and culminating sign of genuine grace is a transformed, persevering, Christlike life — holy practice over time is the most reliable evidence of genuine affections.
    - **Key Points:**
      - This final sign is the most important and most reliable: "By their fruits you will know them" (Matthew 7:16).
      - Holy practice means not a perfect life but a life in which the habitual direction is toward God and godliness, with genuine repentance when sin occurs.
      - It must be sustained over time — not a momentary reformation after a dramatic experience, but a life characterized by love, obedience, and faithfulness across years and decades.
      - Edwards quotes Matthew 7:21 — "Not everyone who says to me 'Lord, Lord' will enter the kingdom of heaven, but the one who does the will of my Father" — as the definitive statement.
    - **Defined Terms:**
      - **Holy Practice:** The habitual, sustained conduct of a regenerate life — not moral perfection but consistent growth in love toward God and neighbor, with genuine sorrow and repentance when falling short.
      - **Christian Practice:** Edwards's phrase for the life-long pattern of obedience, as distinguished from periodic episodes of religious feeling or behavior.
    - **Takeaway:** The test of genuine religion is not the intensity of the experience in the meeting house but the character of the life in the household and the marketplace — and this is a test that takes time to administer.
