## Summary
Hebrews is the Bible's most sustained OT-to-NT theological argument. Its theme is the supremacy of Christ — superior to angels, to Moses, to the Levitical priesthood. The new covenant is better than the old; Christ's one sacrifice is better than endless animal sacrifices. The great hall of faith (ch. 11) motivates endurance; the practical exhortations are inseparable from the theology.

## Notable People
| Person | Role | Why They Matter |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| [[4. People/Abraham\|Abraham]] | Primary example of faith in Hebrews 11 | He obeyed when called to go, not knowing where he was going; he offered Isaac in faith, concluding God was able to raise the dead — a forward look at resurrection |
| [[4. People/Moses\|Moses]] | Second major example in Hebrews 11 | He chose to be mistreated with the people of God rather than enjoy the fleeting pleasures of sin; by faith he kept the Passover, looking ahead to Christ |
| [[4. People/Rahab\|Rahab]] | Example of faith among the unlikely | A Canaanite prostitute who hid the spies by faith; her inclusion in Hebrews 11 underscores that faith — not ethnicity or status — is what God counts |
| [[4. People/Noah\|Noah]] | Builder of the ark by faith | Acting on divine warning about things not yet seen, he condemned the world and became an heir of the righteousness that comes by faith |

*Note: The "Hall of Faith" in ch. 11 also includes Abel, Enoch, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel, and the prophets — all testifying that the pattern of faith-before-fulfillment runs through the whole OT.*
