## Summary

Malachi is the final prophet of the Old Testament canon, writing roughly a century after the return from exile. The initial enthusiasm of restoration has faded. The priests are corrupt, offering blemished sacrifices; the people are intermarrying with pagans, divorcing their wives, and withholding tithes. God's people have grown spiritually cold. Malachi takes the unusual form of a series of disputations — God makes a claim, the people push back ("How?"), and God answers. The book ends with a forward look: a messenger will prepare the way (fulfilled in John the Baptist), and "Elijah" will come before the great and terrible Day of the Lord (also fulfilled in John). After Malachi, four centuries of prophetic silence follow before John's voice breaks it in the wilderness.
