## Summary

The first of the Pastoral Epistles, written to Paul's young co-worker Timothy who has been left in Ephesus to deal with false teachers and to establish healthy church order. Paul addresses who should lead, how churches should worship, and how to care for widows, elders, and slaves. The letter's opening and closing return to the same alarm: certain teachers are spreading "myths and endless genealogies" that promote speculation rather than godly living. Timothy is called to stay, teach sound doctrine, and be an example to the believers. One of the NT's most-quoted texts on Scripture's inspiration appears in the companion letter, 2 Timothy 3 — but 1 Timothy sets the stage for why sound doctrine matters so practically.
