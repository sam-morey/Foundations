# Numbers — Overview

**Author:** Moses (traditional)
**Date:** ~15th–13th century BC
**Genre:** Narrative / Law
**Key Themes:** [[6. Themes/Faith & Trust]], [[6. Themes/Judgment]], [[6. Themes/The Promised Land & Rest]], [[6. Themes/Covenant]]

## Summary
Numbers records Israel's 40-year wilderness journey between Sinai and the plains of Moab. The central crisis is the failure to trust God and enter the land (ch. 13–14), which results in a generation dying in the wilderness. It is a story of grace and judgment, doubt and faithfulness.

## Notable People
| Person | Role | Why They Matter |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| [[4. People/Moses\|Moses]] | Leader and intercessor | Bears the burden of leading a rebellious people through the wilderness; his intercession at critical moments (chs. 14, 16) keeps God's judgment from consuming Israel |
| [[4. People/Aaron\|Aaron]] | High priest | His priesthood is confirmed through the budding staff (ch. 17); he and Moses both fail at Meribah (ch. 20), which bars them from entering the land |
| [[4. People/Miriam\|Miriam]] | Prophetess | Her death in ch. 20 marks the end of the wilderness generation; her challenge to Moses' authority (ch. 12) and its consequences illustrate God's sovereignty over leadership |
| [[4. People/Joshua\|Joshua]] | Military leader and Moses' successor | One of only two of the faithless generation's scouts to trust God (ch. 14); he is commissioned to succeed Moses (ch. 27) |
| Caleb | Faithful spy alongside Joshua | His wholehearted trust at Kadesh-barnea (ch. 14) means he alone among the Exodus generation (besides Joshua) enters the land; his faith sustained over 40 years |
