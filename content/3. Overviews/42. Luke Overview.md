# Luke — Overview

**Author:** Luke, a physician and companion of Paul
**Date:** ~60–70 AD
**Genre:** Gospel
**Key Themes:** [[6. Themes/Redemption & Salvation]], [[6. Themes/Israel & The Gentiles]], [[6. Themes/Grace & Mercy]], [[6. Themes/The Holy Spirit]], [[6. Themes/Prayer & Worship]], [[6. Themes/Justice & Righteousness]]

## Summary
Luke presents Jesus as the Savior of the world — with special attention to the poor, the outsider, women, Samaritans, and Gentiles. The Holy Spirit is prominent from birth narratives through Pentecost (see Acts). Luke's Jesus prays constantly and has compassion for those on the margins. Luke–Acts forms a two-part work on the Spirit-empowered advance of God's Kingdom.

## Notable People
| Person | Role | Why They Matter |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| [[4. People/Jesus\|Jesus]] | Savior of the world | Luke's Jesus is especially attentive to the outsider, the poor, and the sinner — the Son of Man came "to seek and to save the lost" (19:10) |
| [[4. People/Mary (Mother of Jesus)\|Mary]] | Mother of Jesus | Her Magnificat (ch. 1) is the theological heart of the birth narrative — a hymn of reversal where the mighty are brought low and the humble are lifted |
| [[4. People/John the Baptist\|John the Baptist]] | Forerunner of the Messiah | His birth and mission are announced before Jesus', setting up a parallel that highlights Jesus' greater greatness |
| [[4. People/Zacchaeus\|Zacchaeus]] | Tax collector in Jericho | His story (ch. 19) is Luke's clearest picture of salvation: the excluded and despised are the very ones Jesus came to find |
| [[4. People/Mary Magdalene\|Mary Magdalene]] | Faithful follower of Jesus | Among the women who follow Jesus from Galilee and are first at the empty tomb; she and others support Jesus' ministry out of their own means |
| Zechariah | Priest; father of John the Baptist | His unbelief at the angel's announcement and subsequent restoration through Benedictus frame the opening of Luke's narrative |
| Elizabeth | Mother of John the Baptist | The first person in Luke to recognize the Messiah — filled with the Spirit, she cries out "Blessed are you among women!" |
| Simeon | Righteous man in the Temple | Moved by the Spirit to the Temple at just the right moment, he holds the infant Jesus and declares "my eyes have seen your salvation" |
| Anna | Prophetess in the Temple | She never leaves the Temple, fasting and praying; her testimony about the child anticipates the Spirit-led witness that will characterize the whole Luke–Acts story |
| Pilate | Roman governor | He three times declares Jesus innocent but ultimately yields to the crowd — Luke emphasizes this triple verdict to underscore that Jesus died as an innocent man |
| Herod Antipas | Tetrarch of Galilee | He had wanted to see Jesus, but when given the chance he merely mocks him; his collaboration with Pilate ironically becomes a moment of reconciliation between the two men |
