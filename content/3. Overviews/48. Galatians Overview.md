## Summary

Called "the Magna Carta of Christian liberty," Galatians is Paul's most urgent and polemical letter. The churches of Galatia had been visited by teachers insisting that Gentile believers must be circumcised and keep the Mosaic Law to be fully justified before God. Paul's response is electric: there is no other gospel. He reviews his own conversion and confrontation with Peter to establish that justification is by faith in Christ alone, not by works of the law. He traces the covenant back to Abraham — who believed God before circumcision existed — to show that faith, not law-keeping, was always the means of right standing with God. The letter closes with the famous fruit of the Spirit (ch. 5) and the cross as the only ground of boasting (ch. 6).
