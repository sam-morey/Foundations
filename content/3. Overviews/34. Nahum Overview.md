# Nahum — Overview

**Author:** Nahum the Elkoshite | **Date:** ~663–612 BC | **Genre:** Prophetic | **Key Themes:** [[6. Themes/Judgment|Judgment]]

---

## Summary

About a century after Jonah's mission to Nineveh produced a remarkable repentance, Nineveh has returned to its brutal violence and imperial arrogance. Nahum delivers God's verdict: the great Assyrian capital will fall and will not rise again. The book opens with one of the Old Testament's most vivid portraits of God's character — slow to anger, but a consuming fire to his enemies (1:2–8). The rest of the book is a fierce, almost poetic description of Nineveh's coming siege and total destruction. Historically, Nineveh fell to the Babylonians and Medes in 612 BC, exactly as Nahum foretold. The book shows that God's patience has limits, and that oppressive empires are not permanent.
