# Acts — Overview

**Author:** Luke
**Date:** ~62–70 AD
**Genre:** Historical Narrative
**Key Themes:** [[6. Themes/The Holy Spirit]], [[6. Themes/Kingdom of God]], [[6. Themes/Israel & The Gentiles]], [[6. Themes/Redemption & Salvation]], [[6. Themes/Prophecy & Fulfillment]]

## Summary
Acts (formally "Acts of the Apostles" — or better, "Acts of the Holy Spirit") follows the Spirit-empowered spread of the gospel from Jerusalem to the ends of the earth. It is the sequel to Luke and fulfills Jesus' commission (1:8). Key movements: Jerusalem → Judea/Samaria → the Gentile world.

## Notable People
| Person | Role | Why They Matter |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| [[4. People/Peter\|Peter]] | Apostle and leader of the Jerusalem church | He preaches the first sermon at Pentecost (ch. 2), opens the door to the Gentiles via Cornelius (ch. 10), and sets the tone for the Jerusalem Council (ch. 15) |
| [[4. People/Paul\|Paul]] | Apostle to the Gentiles | His conversion (ch. 9) is the pivot of Acts; three missionary journeys and a final voyage to Rome carry the gospel to the heart of the empire |
| [[4. People/Stephen\|Stephen]] | First Christian martyr | His tour of salvation history (ch. 7) is the longest speech in Acts; his death scatters the church and inadvertently launches the Gentile mission |
| [[4. People/Lydia\|Lydia]] | First European convert; merchant in Philippi | Her household baptism (ch. 16) marks the gospel's first foothold in Europe; her home becomes the base for the Philippian church |
| [[4. People/John the Apostle\|John]] | Apostle; companion of Peter in Jerusalem | He accompanies Peter in the early Jerusalem ministry, including the healing at the Temple gate (ch. 3) and the Samaritan mission (ch. 8) |
| Barnabas | Encourager; Paul's first missionary partner | He vouches for Paul before the suspicious Jerusalem apostles (ch. 9) and joins him on the first journey; his generosity sets the tone for the early community |
| Silas | Paul's second missionary partner | He accompanies Paul on the second journey through Macedonia and Greece; his Roman citizenship, like Paul's, proves strategically important |
| Timothy | Young co-worker of Paul | Recruited by Paul at Lystra (ch. 16), he becomes Paul's most trusted delegate and travels with him through much of the second and third journeys |
