## Summary

The most personal and emotionally raw of Paul's letters. Between writing 1 Corinthians and this letter, a painful visit had occurred and "super-apostles" had invaded Corinth, undermining Paul's authority by pointing to his unimpressive appearance and suffering-filled life. Paul responds not by defending himself in worldly terms, but by embracing his weakness as the very place where Christ's power is displayed. The result is a theology of suffering and ministry without parallel in the NT: treasure in jars of clay (4:7), the ministry of reconciliation (5:18), "my grace is sufficient" (12:9). Chapters 8–9 also contain Paul's most extensive teaching on generous giving.
