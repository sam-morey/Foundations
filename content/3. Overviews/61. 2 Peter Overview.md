## Summary

Almost certainly Peter's final letter before his martyrdom, 2 Peter addresses a different threat than 1 Peter. There the danger was external persecution; here it is internal corruption — false teachers who are distorting the faith and indulging immorality. Peter insists that Scripture did not come from human will but from people moved by the Holy Spirit (1:21). He warns of coming judgment using OT examples — the angels who sinned, the flood, Sodom — and promises that false teachers will face the same. The letter closes with one of the NT's most remarkable statements about divine patience: the Lord is not slow to fulfill his promise; he is giving time for repentance. "A thousand years is as a day" in his sight.
