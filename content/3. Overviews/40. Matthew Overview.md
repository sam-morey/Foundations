## Summary
Matthew presents Jesus as the fulfillment of Israel's entire story — the new Moses, the true David, Abraham's ultimate offspring. Written primarily for a Jewish-Christian audience, it emphasizes Jesus' fulfillment of OT prophecy (ten "fulfillment formula" quotes) and his teaching authority. The Sermon on the Mount is its centerpiece.

## Notable People
| Person | Role | Why They Matter |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| [[4. People/Jesus\|Jesus]] | The Messiah and Son of God | Matthew presents Jesus as the new Moses, the fulfillment of Israel's entire story, the King whose birth, teaching, death, and resurrection are the hinge of history |
| [[4. People/Mary (Mother of Jesus)\|Mary]] | Mother of Jesus | Her miraculous conception fulfills Isaiah 7:14; she is a model of humble obedience — "Let it be to me according to your word" |
| [[4. People/Joseph (Earthly Father)\|Joseph]] | Earthly father of Jesus | His quiet obedience protects the Messiah in his most vulnerable moments; he is a righteous man who trusts God's word despite impossible circumstances |
| [[4. People/John the Baptist\|John the Baptist]] | Forerunner of the Messiah | The one who prepares the way (Isaiah 40); he baptizes Jesus and calls the nation to repentance |
| [[4. People/Peter\|Peter]] | Chief apostle | His confession "You are the Christ, Son of the living God" is the bedrock of the church's faith; his denial and restoration trace the arc of grace |
| [[4. People/Judas Iscariot\|Judas Iscariot]] | Betrayer of Jesus | His betrayal for thirty silver coins fulfills Zechariah 11:12–13; his tragedy shows that proximity to Jesus is not the same as faith in Jesus |
| Pilate | Roman governor | He declares Jesus innocent yet hands him over to be crucified, illustrating the power of crowd pressure over justice |
| Herod the Great | King of Judea at Jesus' birth | His paranoid slaughter of the innocents (ch. 2) fulfills Jeremiah 31:15 and echoes Pharaoh's massacre in Exodus |
