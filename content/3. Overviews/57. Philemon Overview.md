# Philemon — Overview

**Author:** Paul | **Date:** ~60–62 AD | **Genre:** Epistle (Personal Letter) | **Key Themes:** [[6. Themes/Grace & Mercy|Grace & Mercy]] · [[6. Themes/Love|Love]]

---

## Summary

The shortest of Paul's letters — just 25 verses — and the most personal. Paul writes to Philemon, a wealthy Christian in Colossae, about his runaway slave Onesimus, who has apparently encountered Paul in prison and become a believer. Paul sends Onesimus back to his master with this letter, asking Philemon to receive him — "no longer as a slave, but better than a slave, as a dear brother" (v. 16). Paul offers to pay whatever debt Onesimus owes. The letter is a miniature portrait of the gospel: substitutionary intercession, the imputation of another's debt, reconciliation between estranged parties, and the social revolution that faith in Christ quietly initiates. Paul never directly attacks the institution of slavery, but the logic of his request subtly undermines it from within.
