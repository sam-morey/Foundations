## Summary
2 Chronicles covers Solomon's reign, the building of the Temple, and then the history of the kings of Judah (omitting the northern kingdom) through the exile. Each king is assessed by their faithfulness in worship and covenant loyalty. The book closes with Cyrus's decree to rebuild the Temple — the dawn of hope after exile.

## Notable People
| Person | Role | Why They Matter |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| [[4. People/Solomon\|Solomon]] | King and Temple-dedicator | His prayer at the Temple dedication (ch. 7) and God's famous response — "If my people… humble themselves and pray" — make him the emblem of covenant worship at its height |
| [[4. People/Hezekiah\|Hezekiah]] | Reforming king of Judah | His sweeping reform and the great Passover revival (chs. 29–31) are Chronicles' model of what covenant renewal looks like; his prayer defeats Assyria |
| Josiah | Last great reforming king | His discovery of the Law and radical reform (chs. 34–35) provide the last bright light before the exile; Chronicles holds him up as the ideal Davidic king |
