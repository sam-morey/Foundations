# Hosea — Overview

**Author:** Hosea ben Beeri | **Date:** ~750–720 BC | **Genre:** Prophetic | **Key Themes:** [[Love|Love]] · [[Covenant|Covenant]] · [[Judgment|Judgment]] · [[Grace & Mercy|Grace & Mercy]]

---

## Summary

Hosea prophesies to the northern kingdom of Israel during its final decades before the Assyrian conquest (722 BC). God commands him to marry Gomer, a woman who proves unfaithful — a living parable of Israel's spiritual adultery in chasing other gods. Yet the book's overwhelming message is not judgment but relentless love: God will not give up on his people. The divine grief of chapter 11 ("How can I give you up, O Ephraim?") is one of the most tender portraits of God in all Scripture. The book ends with a call to return and a promise of healing.

## Notable People
| Person | Role | Why They Matter |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| Hosea | Prophet of God's relentless love | Called to marry an unfaithful wife as a living parable; his personal anguish becomes a window into God's own heartbreak over Israel's spiritual adultery and his refusal to give up on his people |
| Gomer | Hosea's unfaithful wife | Her repeated unfaithfulness and Hosea's re-purchase of her (ch. 3) dramatize the central theological claim: God's love pursues, redeems, and restores even the persistently wayward |
