# Daniel — Overview

**Author:** Daniel
**Date:** 6th century BC (events); literary composition debated
**Genre:** Historical Narrative + Apocalyptic
**Key Themes:** [[Kingdom of God]], [[Prophecy & Fulfillment]], [[Faith & Trust]], [[Messiah & Christ]], [[Judgment]]

## Summary
Daniel lives in Babylon under Nebuchadnezzar and successive kings, maintaining faithful devotion to God under persecution. The second half (chs. 7–12) contains visions of world empires and the coming Kingdom of God — including the "Son of Man" figure (ch. 7) who receives an eternal kingdom. Jesus frequently echoes Daniel's imagery.

## Notable People
| Person | Role | Why They Matter |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| [[4. People/Daniel\|Daniel]] | Exile and visionary prophet | His faithful refusal to compromise under successive pagan empires, and his visionary reception of the "Son of Man" imagery (ch. 7), make him one of the most prophetically significant figures in the OT; Jesus draws heavily on Daniel's language |
| Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego | Daniel's three companions | Their refusal to bow before Nebuchadnezzar's statue — "even if he does not [deliver us]" (ch. 3) — is the Bible's defining statement of defiant trust; their deliverance points to God's power over the most extreme opposition |
| Nebuchadnezzar | King of Babylon | The most powerful ruler of his age; his humiliation and eventual acknowledgment of God (ch. 4) demonstrate that even the mightiest earthly king is subject to the Most High |
