# Sin & The Fall

Sin is humanity's rebellion against God — a fundamental rupture in the relationship between Creator and creature. The Fall in Genesis 3 explains universal human brokenness, and the rest of Scripture traces God's response to it.

## Key Passages

### The Fall
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/01. Genesis/Genesis 3\|Genesis 3]] — The serpent, the forbidden fruit, the first sin, and the curse
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/01. Genesis/Genesis 4\|Genesis 4]] — Cain murders Abel; sin spreads immediately

### Sin's Universal Scope
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/19. Psalms/Psalms 14\|Psalm 14]] — "There is none who does good, not even one"
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/19. Psalms/Psalms 51\|Psalm 51]] — David's confession after adultery and murder
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/23. Isaiah/Isaiah 53\|Isaiah 53]] — "All we like sheep have gone astray"
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/45. Romans/Romans 1\|Romans 1]] — Universal suppression of truth and moral decline
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/45. Romans/Romans 3\|Romans 3]] — All have sinned and fall short of God's glory
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/45. Romans/Romans 5\|Romans 5]] — Sin and death entered through one man (Adam)
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/45. Romans/Romans 7\|Romans 7]] — The inner struggle with sin

### The Consequences of Sin
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/01. Genesis/Genesis 3\|Genesis 3]] — Spiritual death, broken relationships, physical labor and pain
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/23. Isaiah/Isaiah 59\|Isaiah 59]] — Iniquities separate from God
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/45. Romans/Romans 6\|Romans 6]] — The wages of sin is death
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/49. Ephesians/Ephesians 2\|Ephesians 2]] — Humanity dead in trespasses and sins

### God's Response to Sin
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/01. Genesis/Genesis 3\|Genesis 3:15]] — First promise of a Redeemer (*protevangelium*)
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/03. Leviticus/Leviticus 16\|Leviticus 16]] — Day of Atonement; covering sin
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/23. Isaiah/Isaiah 53\|Isaiah 53]] — The Servant bears the sin of many
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/43. John/John 1\|John 1]] — Behold the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/62. I John/I John 1\|1 John 1]] — Confession and cleansing from sin

## Supplemental Reading
- [[5. Supplemental Content/C.S. Lewis/Mere Christianity|Mere Christianity]] (Lewis) — sin as moral failure, not mere social dysfunction; clear and compelling
- [[5. Supplemental Content/Paradise Lost|Paradise Lost]] (Milton) — the greatest literary treatment of the Fall and its cosmic consequences
- [[5. Supplemental Content/Confessions|Confessions]] (Augustine) — sin narrated from the inside; the restless heart before it finds rest in God
- [[5. Supplemental Content/C.S. Lewis/The Screwtape Letters|The Screwtape Letters]] (Lewis) — temptation as seen from the demonic side; subtle and illuminating
- [[5. Supplemental Content/John Mark Comer/Live No Lies|Live No Lies]] (Comer) — the world, the flesh, and the devil as contemporary framings of ancient sin patterns
- [[5. Supplemental Content/Orthodoxy|Orthodoxy]] (Chesterton) — original sin as the one Christian doctrine empirically verifiable; the Fall as the only adequate explanation for human nature
- [[5. Supplemental Content/The Reason for God|The Reason for God]] (Keller) — sin as a structural disorder of the soul that operates below conscious choice
