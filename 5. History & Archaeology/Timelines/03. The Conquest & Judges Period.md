## Biblical Events
- ~1406 BC — Joshua leads Israel across the Jordan; miraculous crossing at flood stage (Josh 3)
- ~1406 BC — Fall of Jericho; the walls collapse; Rahab and her household spared (Josh 6)
- ~1400 BC — Defeat of the southern coalition at Aijalon; sun stands still (Josh 10)
- ~1400–1380 BC — Conquest of Canaan; division of the land among the twelve tribes (Josh 11–21)
- ~1380 BC — Death of Joshua; the covenant renewal ceremony at Shechem (Josh 24)
- ~1375–1050 BC — The Judges period: cyclical pattern of apostasy, oppression, repentance, deliverance
- ~1209 BC — Deborah and Barak defeat Sisera and the Canaanite coalition (Judg 4–5)
- ~1162 BC — Gideon defeats the Midianites with 300 men (Judg 7)
- ~1105–1085 BC — Samson judges Israel against the Philistines (Judg 13–16)
- ~1100 BC — Ruth the Moabitess accompanies Naomi to Bethlehem; marries Boaz; ancestor of David (Ruth)

## World History Context

The period from approximately 1200–1150 BC witnessed one of the most catastrophic and widespread civilizational collapses in recorded history — the Bronze Age Collapse. Virtually every major palace economy in the eastern Mediterranean was destroyed or severely disrupted within the space of a few decades: the Mycenaean palatial centers at Mycenae, Tiryns, Pylos, and Thebes were burned; the Hittite Empire, one of the great superpowers of the Late Bronze Age, was destroyed around 1180 BC, its capital Hattusa burned and abandoned; Ugarit, the great cosmopolitan trading city on the Syrian coast, was destroyed around 1185 BC and never rebuilt; even mighty Egypt was attacked and severely weakened.

The primary agents of this destruction were the Sea Peoples — a loose confederation of migrants, raiders, and displaced populations moving through the eastern Mediterranean, most likely originating from the Aegean, Anatolia, and the Greek world. Among the Sea Peoples were the Peleset — almost certainly the biblical Philistines — who attempted to invade Egypt under Ramesses III (depicted in stunning relief at Medinet Habu, c. 1175 BC) and, when repulsed, settled on the southern coastal plain of Canaan in the five cities of Gaza, Ashkelon, Ashdod, Ekron, and Gath. This placed them in direct and lasting conflict with Israel throughout the judges and early monarchy periods.

The transition from Bronze Age to Iron Age transformed warfare and agriculture across the ancient world. The Philistines appear to have monopolized iron technology in Canaan for a period (cf. 1 Sam 13:19–22), giving them a significant military advantage over Israel. The Phoenician city-states — Tyre, Sidon, Byblos — survived the Bronze Age Collapse and went on to establish a maritime trading network across the Mediterranean, eventually founding Carthage (~814 BC) and transmitting the Phoenician alphabet to Greece. Israel, settling in the hill country of Canaan, developed a distinctive material culture identifiable archaeologically by the four-room house form, collar-rim storage jars, and rock-cut cisterns that allowed settlement in previously uninhabited highland regions.

## Dominant Powers & Their Impact on Israel
| Power | Dates | Impact on Israel/Bible |
|-------|-------|------------------------|
| Canaanite city-states | 1400–1000 BC | Primary opponents in the conquest; their religious practices (Baal worship, temple prostitution, child sacrifice) were the chief temptation to apostasy throughout Judges |
| Philistines (Sea Peoples) | 1175–700 BC | Settled coastal Canaan after Bronze Age Collapse; iron-age military superiors; oppressed Israel for much of the Judges/Saul period; captured the Ark (1 Sam 4–6) |
| Egypt (weakened) | 1150–1000 BC | Post-Bronze-Age-Collapse Egypt was too weak to control Canaan effectively; created a power vacuum Israel entered |
| Midianites | 1162 BC | Oppressed Israel from the Transjordan desert; defeated by Gideon (Judg 6–8) |
| Ammonites & Moabites | Various | Repeated oppressors in the Transjordan; provided context for Jephthah, Ehud, and the book of Ruth |

## Key Archaeological Evidence
- **Destruction Layer at Hazor (Stratum XIII)** — Hazor, the largest Canaanite city in Canaan, shows a massive destruction layer (~1230–1200 BC) consistent with a violent conquest; the royal palace was burned, its statues deliberately decapitated — an act matching Joshua 11:10–13's description of burning Hazor
- **Destruction Layers at Lachish (Level VI)** — Lachish shows destruction ~1150 BC; though the exact date is debated in relation to the conquest, the destruction of Canaanite Lachish corresponds to the biblical account in Josh 10
- **Merneptah Stele** (1208 BC) — Establishes that Israel was a people present in Canaan before 1200 BC, directly relevant to dating the conquest and settlement
- **Philistine Bichrome Pottery at Ashdod, Ashkelon, and Tel Miqne/Ekron** — Distinctive Mycenaean-style decorated pottery marks the arrival and spread of Philistine settlement in the coastal plain; Ekron (Tel Miqne) became one of the largest cities in Canaan
- **Four-Room Israelite House Form** — Widespread in the central hill country from ~1200 BC onward; a distinctive architectural pattern associated with Israelite settlements at sites like Ai, Raddana, and Giloh; consistent with the emergence of a new population group in the highlands
- **Medinet Habu Reliefs** (c. 1175 BC, Luxor, Egypt) — Temple reliefs of Ramesses III depicting his battles against the Sea Peoples on land and sea; includes depictions of the Philistines with feathered headdresses; the primary visual record of the Sea Peoples' invasion
- **Shiloh Excavations (Khirbet Seilun)** — Danish excavations confirm a significant destruction of Shiloh ~1050 BC, consistent with the biblical account of the Ark's capture and the apparent destruction of Shiloh referenced in Jer 7:12

## Notable Figures (Biblical & Historical)
| Figure | Role | Significance |
|--------|------|--------------|
| Joshua | Military leader; successor to Moses | Leads the conquest of Canaan; divides the land; leads covenant renewal at Shechem (Josh 24) |
| Rahab | Canaanite prostitute in Jericho | Faith under enemy fire; spared at the conquest; ancestor of David and Jesus (Matt 1:5) |
| Deborah | Judge and prophetess | Only female judge; military leader alongside Barak; Song of Deborah (Judg 5) is among the oldest Hebrew poetry |
| Gideon | Judge | Defeats Midianites with 300 men; fleece sign; later stumbles into idolatry with the ephod |
| Samson | Judge | Nazirite warrior; embodies Israel's spiritual condition — great potential, squandered through compromise |
| Ruth | Moabite widow | Model of covenant loyalty (hesed); great-grandmother of David; Gentile included in the messianic line |
| Ramesses III | Pharaoh of Egypt | Repelled the Sea Peoples invasion; weakened Egypt and allowed Philistines to settle Canaan |
