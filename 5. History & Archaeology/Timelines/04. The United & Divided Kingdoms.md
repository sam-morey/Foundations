## Biblical Events
- ~1050 BC — Samuel anoints Saul as Israel's first king in response to the people's demand (1 Sam 9–10)
- ~1010 BC — David anointed king of Judah after Saul's death at Gilboa; later king of all Israel (2 Sam 2, 5)
- ~1000 BC — David captures Jerusalem (Jebus); makes it his capital; the Ark of the Covenant brought to Zion (2 Sam 5–6)
- ~990 BC — Davidic covenant: God promises an eternal dynasty and a son who will build the temple (2 Sam 7)
- ~970 BC — Solomon becomes king; builds the First Temple on Mount Moriah (1 Kgs 6–8)
- ~930 BC — Death of Solomon; the kingdom splits under Rehoboam (south) and Jeroboam I (north) (1 Kgs 12)
- ~925 BC — Pharaoh Shoshenq I (Shishak) invades and plunders Jerusalem and the northern kingdom (1 Kgs 14:25–26)
- ~885–874 BC — Omri founds Samaria as the northern capital; builds a powerful dynasty (1 Kgs 16)
- ~874–853 BC — Ahab and Jezebel; the confrontation with Elijah at Mount Carmel (1 Kgs 18)
- ~841 BC — Jehu's revolt; Ahab's dynasty ends; Jehu pays tribute to Assyria's Shalmaneser III
- ~722 BC — Assyria under Sargon II captures Samaria; the northern kingdom of Israel exiled and destroyed (2 Kgs 17)

## World History Context

The rise of the Israelite monarchy coincided with a period of relative weakness among the great ancient powers — Egypt had never fully recovered from the Bronze Age Collapse, the Hittites were gone, and the Neo-Assyrian Empire had not yet reached its full territorial expansion. This brief window of relative power vacuum in the Levant made possible the remarkable achievements of David and Solomon: a unified kingdom stretching from the Euphrates to the border of Egypt (1 Kgs 4:21), a thriving trade network with Phoenicia (Hiram of Tyre), Arabia (the Queen of Sheba), and the Mediterranean world, and the construction of the Jerusalem Temple as the cultic center of the ancient world.

Phoenician civilization flourished alongside the Israelite kingdom. The Phoenicians of Tyre and Sidon, descended from the Canaanites, became the master seafarers and traders of the Mediterranean, establishing colonies as far west as Carthage (~814 BC), founded by Dido according to tradition. They transmitted the Phoenician/Canaanite alphabet — the ancestor of the Greek, Latin, and ultimately English alphabets — throughout the ancient world. Solomon's building projects relied heavily on Phoenician craftsmen and materials (cedar from Lebanon, skilled Hiram the bronze-worker from Tyre), and Israelite religious syncretism was repeatedly fueled by Phoenician influence (Jezebel was a Phoenician princess).

In the wider world, the Greek Dark Ages were ending around 900 BC, and the city-states of the Greek world were beginning to organize. Rome was still centuries from its imperial form, but Roman tradition dates its founding to 753 BC — roughly contemporary with the divided kingdom period. In Mesopotamia, the Neo-Assyrian Empire was consolidating its power under a series of aggressive rulers: Ashurnasirpal II (r. 883–859 BC) conducted brutal campaigns westward, and Tiglath-Pileser III (r. 745–727 BC) implemented the policy of mass deportation that would eventually swallow the northern kingdom of Israel.

## Key Archaeological Evidence
- **Tel Dan Stele** (c. 841 BC, Israel Museum, Jerusalem) — Aramaic victory inscription of Hazael of Damascus; contains the phrase "House of David" (bytdwd) — the earliest extra-biblical mention of King David; confirms the Davidic dynasty as a historical reality
- **Mesha Stele / Moabite Stone** (c. 840 BC, Louvre Museum) — Stele of King Mesha of Moab; describes his revolt against Israel and mentions "Omri king of Israel"; confirms the Omride dynasty and Israel's historical domination of Moab (cf. 2 Kgs 3)
- **Shoshenq I's Megiddo Relief and Campaign List** (c. 925 BC, Karnak Temple, Luxor) — Egyptian pharaoh Shoshenq I's (biblical Shishak) military campaign list at Karnak temple records over 150 place-names in Canaan; a fragment of his victory stele was found at Megiddo; directly corroborates 1 Kings 14:25–26
- **Black Obelisk of Shalmaneser III** (c. 841 BC, British Museum) — Assyrian obelisk depicting tribute-bearers; the second panel shows "Iaua son of Omri" — almost certainly King Jehu of Israel — prostrating before the Assyrian king and presenting tribute; earliest visual depiction of a biblical figure
- **Siloam Tunnel Inscription** (c. 700 BC, Istanbul Archaeological Museum) — Six-line Hebrew inscription found in Hezekiah's water tunnel describing the moment the two digging teams met; corroborates 2 Kings 20:20 and 2 Chronicles 32:30
- **Gezer Calendar** (c. 925 BC, Istanbul Archaeological Museum) — Small limestone tablet listing agricultural seasons in early Hebrew script; one of the oldest known Hebrew inscriptions; demonstrates literacy in Israel during the early monarchy
- **Ivory Ivories of Samaria** (9th–8th c. BC, Israel Museum) — Over 500 carved ivory fragments from the Omride palace at Samaria; high-quality Phoenician-style luxury goods; corroborate Amos's condemnations of the "house of ivory" (Amos 3:15) and Ahab's "ivory house" (1 Kgs 22:39)

## Notable Figures (Biblical & Historical)
| Figure | Role | Significance |
|--------|------|--------------|
| Saul | First king of Israel | Tall, promising beginning; disobedience leads to rejection; tragic arc of leadership without full submission to God |
| David | King of Israel; psalmist | Man after God's own heart (1 Sam 13:14); recipient of the eternal covenant; ancestor of the Messiah; author of many Psalms |
| Solomon | King; builder of the Temple; sage | Unparalleled wisdom and wealth; builds the First Temple; his foreign wives lead to syncretism and ultimate division of the kingdom |
| Elijah | Prophet | Confronts Ahab and Baal worship on Mount Carmel; type of John the Baptist (Mal 4:5; Matt 11:14); taken to heaven in a whirlwind |
| Omri | King of northern Israel | Founded Samaria; established a dynasty so influential that Assyrians called Israel "the house of Omri" for a century |
| Ahab | King of northern Israel | Married Jezebel; promoted Baal worship; Elijah's primary opponent; died at Ramoth-Gilead |
| Shoshenq I (Shishak) | Pharaoh of Egypt | First extrabiblical confirmation of a biblical event through his campaign list at Karnak |
| Shalmaneser III | King of Assyria | Black Obelisk shows Jehu paying tribute; first Assyrian king to directly contact Israelite kings |
