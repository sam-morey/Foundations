These notes focus on specific archaeological discoveries — inscriptions, artifacts, manuscripts, and excavation finds — that illuminate, confirm, or contextualize the biblical record. The lens here is the discovery itself rather than the place where it was found.

---

## The Earliest Records

The oldest extra-biblical evidence touching Israel's identity and the world of the Exodus.

- [[Merneptah Stele]] — ~1208 BC; the only ancient Egyptian text naming Israel; "Israel is laid waste, his seed is not"; the earliest extra-biblical mention of Israel by name; Egyptian Museum, Cairo

---

## The Monarchy Period

Inscriptions from the era of Israel's kings — confirming specific rulers, dynasties, and events described in Samuel, Kings, and Chronicles.

- [[Mesha Stele (Moabite Stone)]] — ~840 BC; King Mesha of Moab's victory inscription; names "Omri king of Israel"; mentions YHWH; probable "House of David" reference in line 31; Louvre, Paris
- [[Tel Dan Stele]] — ~841–835 BC; Aramaic victory inscription by Hazael of Damascus; the clearest extra-biblical reference to the Davidic dynasty ("House of David"); Israel Museum, Jerusalem
- [[Siloam Tunnel & Hezekiah's Inscription]] — ~701 BC; Hezekiah's 533-meter rock-cut water tunnel and its 6-line Hebrew inscription; one of the longest ancient Hebrew inscriptions ever found; confirms 2 Kings 20:20; Istanbul Archaeological Museum (inscription); tunnel in situ, Jerusalem
- [[Sennacherib's Prism (Taylor Prism)]] — ~691 BC; Sennacherib's own account of his siege of Judah and Jerusalem; names Hezekiah; conspicuously does not claim Jerusalem's capture — confirming 2 Kings 19:35–36; British Museum, London

---

## The Exile & Return

Manuscripts preserved from the intertestamental period that illuminate the world of the Second Temple and the transmission of Scripture.

- [[Dead Sea Scrolls & Qumran]] — ~150 BC–68 AD; 900+ manuscripts from eleven caves near the Dead Sea; the Great Isaiah Scroll; the Community Rule; oldest biblical manuscripts by 1,000 years; Shrine of the Book, Israel Museum, Jerusalem

---

## The New Testament Era

Physical artifacts that confirm named figures, events, and chronology from the Gospels, Acts, and the letters of Paul.

- [[Pilate Stone]] — ~26–37 AD; the only inscription ever found naming Pontius Pilate; confirms his exact title "Prefect of Judaea"; found at Caesarea Maritima 1961; Israel Museum, Jerusalem
- [[Ossuary of Caiaphas]] — 1st century AD; limestone bone box inscribed "Joseph son of Caiaphas"; the high priest of Jesus's trial; found 1990 in a Jerusalem tomb; Israel Museum, Jerusalem
- [[Gallio Inscription]] — ~52 AD; Emperor Claudius's letter naming "Gallio my friend and proconsul of Achaia"; dates Paul's Corinthian ministry to 50–52 AD with precision; the primary chronological anchor for Pauline chronology; Delphi Archaeological Museum, Greece
