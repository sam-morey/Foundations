# Zechariah — Overview

**Author:** Zechariah ben Berechiah | **Date:** ~520–480 BC | **Genre:** Prophetic | **Key Themes:** [[6. Themes/Messiah & Christ|Messiah & Christ]] · [[6. Themes/Prophecy & Fulfillment|Prophecy & Fulfillment]] · [[6. Themes/Kingdom of God|Kingdom of God]]

---

## Summary

The most messianic of the minor prophets, Zechariah is cited more in the New Testament passion narratives than almost any other book. Writing alongside Haggai in the post-exilic period, he encourages the returned community with eight dramatic night visions and then, in chapters 9–14, delivers oracles about the coming King. These later chapters contain an astonishing cluster of details fulfilled in Jesus: the king riding on a donkey (9:9), the betrayal price of thirty pieces of silver (11:12–13), the sheep scattered when the shepherd is struck (13:7), and the one who is pierced and mourned (12:10). The book ends with a vision of the Lord becoming King over all the earth (14:9).

| The Coming King | 9–11 | The king on a donkey; the thirty pieces of silver; the rejected shepherd |

## Notable People

| Person | Role | Why They Matter |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| Zechariah | Most messianic of the minor prophets | Eight night visions and oracles that include the king riding on a donkey, the thirty pieces of silver, and the one pierced — all quoted in the passion narratives of the Gospels |
| Zerubbabel | Governor; subject of the fourth vision | Named as the one who will complete the Temple by God's Spirit; carries the Davidic hope of the post-exilic community |
| Joshua | High priest; subject of the third vision | Cleansed from filthy garments and re-robed — a picture of priestly restoration and, typologically, the work of the coming Branch |
