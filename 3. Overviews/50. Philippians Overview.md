## Summary

The "Joy Letter" — written from prison, yet saturated with the word "joy" or "rejoice" more than almost any other NT letter. Paul writes to the Philippian church, the first congregation he founded in Europe, calling them his "joy and crown" (4:1). The letter's theological centerpiece is the Christ hymn of chapter 2, one of the New Testament's most exalted passages: Christ, though in the form of God, humbled himself to the point of death on a cross, and God therefore exalted him to the name above every name. This pattern of humility before exaltation becomes the model for the Christian life. The letter closes with Paul's famous declaration of contentment learned in any circumstance through Christ who strengthens him.
