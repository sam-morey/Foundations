## Summary

The most practical letter in the New Testament — wisdom writing in the tradition of Proverbs, laser-focused on the lived implications of faith. James was once a skeptic during Jesus's ministry (John 7:5), but the resurrection transformed him into the pillar leader of the Jerusalem church. He writes to Jewish believers scattered across the Roman world, addressing the gap between what they profess and how they live. The letter's most famous passage — "faith without works is dead" (2:17) — has sometimes been read as contradicting Paul, but James targets empty verbal profession, not Paul's opponents who sought to add law-keeping to grace. James wants to know: does your faith actually do anything?
