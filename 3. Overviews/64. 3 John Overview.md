## Summary

The shortest book in the New Testament (15 verses), 3 John is addressed to a specific individual named Gaius, whom John commends for his hospitality to traveling missionaries — the exact opposite situation from 2 John. But the letter also names Diotrephes, a church leader who "likes to put himself first," refuses to welcome the brothers, and is trying to expel from the church anyone who does. John is on his way to deal with Diotrephes personally. The little letter is a window into real tensions in the early church — the struggle between legitimate authority and the kind of ego-driven leadership that hinders the gospel. Gaius is held up as the counter-model: walk in the truth, do good, imitate what is good.
