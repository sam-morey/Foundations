## Summary
John's Gospel is the most theological of the four. It opens with the cosmic prologue ("In the beginning was the Word") and structures itself around seven signs and seven "I AM" sayings. Its purpose is explicit: "These are written that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name" (20:31).

## Notable People
| Person | Role | Why They Matter |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| [[4. People/Jesus\|Jesus]] | The eternal Word made flesh | John presents Jesus with the highest Christology of the four Gospels — the eternal Son who was with God and was God, whose seven "I AM" sayings echo the divine name |
| [[4. People/Peter\|Peter]] | Apostle and leader | His threefold denial (ch. 18) is undone by a threefold restoration in ch. 21 — "Do you love me? Feed my sheep" — making John's epilogue a story of redemption |
| [[4. People/John the Apostle\|John]] | The Beloved Disciple; author | The unnamed "disciple whom Jesus loved" reclines at the Last Supper, stands at the cross, and outruns Peter to the tomb — a model of intimate discipleship |
| [[4. People/Mary Magdalene\|Mary Magdalene]] | First witness to the resurrection | She comes to the tomb while it is still dark; her encounter with the risen Jesus in the garden (ch. 20) is one of Scripture's most moving moments |
| [[4. People/Martha\|Martha]] | Sister of Lazarus; follower of Jesus | Her conversation with Jesus before her brother's resurrection — "I am the resurrection and the life. Do you believe this?" — is the theological climax of ch. 11 |
| [[4. People/Lazarus\|Lazarus]] | Man raised from the dead | His resurrection (ch. 11) is the seventh and greatest sign, triggering the decision of the Sanhedrin to put Jesus to death |
| [[4. People/Nicodemus\|Nicodemus]] | Pharisee and secret disciple | He comes to Jesus at night in ch. 3; defends Jesus cautiously before the Sanhedrin in ch. 7; and finally helps bury him openly in ch. 19 — a quiet arc of growing courage |
| [[4. People/Thomas\|Thomas]] | Apostle known for doubt and faith | His demand for proof and his eventual confession "My Lord and my God!" (ch. 20) is the Gospel's climactic statement of faith |
| [[4. People/Judas Iscariot\|Judas Iscariot]] | Betrayer of Jesus | John alone notes that Judas was a thief who pilfered the common purse; Satan enters him at the Last Supper and he goes out into the night |
| Pilate | Roman governor | His extended dialogue with Jesus in ch. 18–19 is the most developed of the four Gospels; he asks "What is truth?" while Truth himself stands before him |
