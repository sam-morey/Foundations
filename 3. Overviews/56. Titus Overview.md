# Titus — Overview

**Author:** Paul | **Date:** ~62–67 AD | **Genre:** Pastoral Epistle | **Key Themes:** [[6. Themes/Wisdom|Wisdom]] · [[6. Themes/Grace & Mercy|Grace & Mercy]] · [[6. Themes/Covenant|Covenant]]

## Summary

Paul writes to Titus, his co-worker left on the island of Crete to appoint elders and correct disorder. Like 1 Timothy, it addresses the qualifications for church leaders and the problem of false teachers. But Titus has its own distinctive contribution: chapters 2 and 3 contain two of the most concentrated statements of grace-motivated ethics in the NT. Sound doctrine does not float free — it produces a certain kind of person, and the grace of God "trains us to renounce ungodliness and worldly passions, and to live self-controlled, upright, and godly lives" (2:12). The great statement on regeneration and renewal by the Spirit (3:5) is one of the clearest NT texts on the new birth.
