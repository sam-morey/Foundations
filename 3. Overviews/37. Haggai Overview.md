## Summary

Haggai is one of the three post-exilic prophets (with Zechariah and Malachi). The exiles have returned from Babylon, but the Temple — the symbol of God's presence with his people — lies in ruins sixteen years after the return began. Meanwhile, the people have built their own houses and are focused on personal comfort. Haggai delivers four short oracles in 520 BC calling the governor Zerubbabel and the high priest Joshua to lead the Temple rebuilding. His central challenge is a question about priorities: "Is it a time for you to dwell in your paneled houses while this house lies in ruins?" (1:4). His promise is that God himself will provide the resources, that the latter glory of the rebuilt Temple will surpass the former, and that God is with them.

## Notable People

| Person | Role | Why They Matter |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| Haggai | Post-exilic prophet of Temple rebuilding | Calls the returned exiles to reorder their priorities and complete the Temple; his message that God is with them encourages a discouraged community to resume what they had abandoned |
| Zerubbabel | Governor of Judah, Temple builder | The Davidic heir who leads the Temple rebuilding; God calls him his "signet ring" — a messianic designation pointing forward to the greater son of David |
