# 1 Corinthians — Overview

## Summary

Written to a divided, worldly church in the cosmopolitan port city of Corinth, 1 Corinthians is Paul's most wide-ranging letter in its practical scope. The church was fracturing over celebrity preachers, tolerating sexual immorality, suing each other in pagan courts, and misusing spiritual gifts. Paul addresses each crisis in turn, always grounding his answers in the gospel. The result is one of the richest single letters in the New Testament: it contains the earliest written account of the Lord's Supper (ch. 11), the great chapter on spiritual gifts and the body of Christ (ch. 12), the hymn to love (ch. 13), and the fullest treatment of the resurrection in the NT (ch. 15). Its opening is the book's key: the cross of Christ, which the world calls foolishness, is the wisdom and power of God.

## Structure

| Section | Chapters | Content |
|---------|----------|---------|
| Divisions and the Cross | 1–4 | Factions; wisdom of the world vs. the cross; Paul as humble servant |
| Ethical Issues | 5–7 | Sexual immorality; lawsuits; marriage and singleness |
| Freedom and Conscience | 8–10 | Food offered to idols; the weaker brother; "all things are lawful but not all are helpful" |
| Corporate Worship | 11–14 | Head coverings; the Lord's Supper; the body and gifts; love is greatest; tongues and prophecy |
| The Resurrection | 15 | Christ's resurrection as the ground of ours; the order of resurrection; "Death is swallowed up in victory" |
| Closing | 16 | The collection for Jerusalem; travel plans; final greetings |
