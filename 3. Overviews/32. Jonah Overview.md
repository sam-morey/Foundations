## Summary

Jonah is unique among the prophets: the book contains almost no oracles, and the prophet himself is the problem. Called to preach to Israel's enemies in Nineveh, Jonah flees in the opposite direction, is swallowed by a great fish, prays from its belly, and is delivered. He goes to Nineveh, preaches a five-word message, and the entire city repents in sackcloth — the most successful evangelistic mission in history. Then Jonah sits under a plant, furious that God showed mercy to Israel's enemies. God's question at the end — "Should I not be concerned about Nineveh?" — is the point of the whole book: God's compassion is wider than any prophet's nationalism.

Jesus uses Jonah as a sign of his own death and resurrection (Matt 12:39–41).
