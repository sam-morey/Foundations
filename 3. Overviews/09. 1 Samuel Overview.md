## Summary
1 Samuel records the transition from the period of judges to the monarchy. It follows Samuel the prophet-judge, Saul the failed king, and the rise of David — the shepherd-king after God's own heart.

## Notable People
| Person | Role | Why They Matter |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| [[4. People/Hannah\|Hannah]] | Mother of Samuel | Her prayer and song (chs. 1–2) model faithful intercession; her song of reversal anticipates Mary's Magnificat and the coming of a king who exalts the lowly |
| [[4. People/Samuel\|Samuel]] | Prophet, priest, and judge | The hinge figure between the age of judges and the monarchy; he anoints both Saul and David, demonstrating that true kingship is God's to give and take |
| [[4. People/Saul\|Saul]] | Israel's first king | Chosen for his outward appearance, he repeatedly disobeys God; his rejection illustrates that God demands covenant faithfulness, not ritual compliance |
| [[4. People/David\|David]] | Shepherd-king after God's own heart | Anointed in secret (ch. 16) while still a boy; his defeat of Goliath and rise through suffering model the pattern of God's chosen servant — rejected before exalted |
| Jonathan | Crown prince and David's covenant friend | His loyalty to David over his own dynastic interest is one of the Bible's greatest portraits of covenant friendship; he foreshadows those who willingly yield place to God's chosen |
