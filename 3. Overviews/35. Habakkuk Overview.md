## Summary

Habakkuk stands out among the prophets: rather than delivering God's word to the people, he argues with God. He opens with a complaint — why does God tolerate injustice in Judah? God's answer shocks him: he is raising up the violent Babylonians to punish his own people. This produces a second complaint even more anguished than the first. How can a holy God use a nation more wicked than Judah as his instrument of judgment? God's second answer is the book's theological heart: "The righteous shall live by his faith" (2:4). This single verse, quoted three times in the New Testament (Romans, Galatians, Hebrews), becomes a cornerstone of Reformation theology. The book ends not with resolution but with radical trust — Habakkuk declares that he will rejoice in God even if everything fails (3:17–19).
