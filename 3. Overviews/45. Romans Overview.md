## Summary
Romans is Paul's most systematic theological letter — his fullest statement of the gospel. It demonstrates that all humanity stands condemned, that God's righteousness is revealed in the gospel through faith, and that this salvation has cosmic and historical implications (Israel and the Gentiles, creation's renewal). Chapters 12–16 apply this theologically dense gospel to everyday life.

| The Jewish Question | 9–11 | God's faithfulness to Israel; the mystery of the Gentiles |
| Christian Ethics | 12–16 | Living sacrifice; love; government; the strong and weak |
