## Summary

Written in old age, 1 John distills a lifetime of witness to Jesus into a letter of assurance and discernment. A proto-Gnostic movement had left John's community, claiming spiritual enlightenment while denying the incarnation and living immorally. John counters by giving three interlocking tests of genuine Christianity: right belief (confessing that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh), right behavior (keeping his commandments), and right love (loving the brothers and sisters). The letter's theological peaks are among the NT's highest: "God is light, and in him is no darkness at all" (1:5) and "God is love" (4:8, 16) — the latter the only place in Scripture where God's very being is defined as love.
