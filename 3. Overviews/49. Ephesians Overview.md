## Summary

Written from prison, Ephesians is the most cosmic and soaring of Paul's letters. Where Romans traces the logic of individual salvation and Galatians defends it against attack, Ephesians steps back to reveal God's eternal purpose: to bring all things in heaven and on earth together under Christ as head (1:10). The first three chapters are almost entirely praise and prayer — one long theological breath of wonder at what God has done in uniting Jews and Gentiles into one new humanity through the cross. The final three chapters apply this theology to household relationships, church unity, and the spiritual battle every believer faces. The famous "armor of God" passage (ch. 6) closes the letter.
