# Boaz

**Testament:** Old Testament
**Era:** The Judges
**Appears In:** [[3. Book Overviews/08. Ruth Overview|Ruth]], [[3. Book Overviews/40. Matthew Overview|Matthew]]

## Who They Are
Boaz is a wealthy and respected landowner in Bethlehem, a relative of Naomi's deceased husband. When Ruth comes to glean in his fields, Boaz extends extraordinary generosity that goes well beyond legal obligation. He fulfills the role of kinsman-redeemer, marrying Ruth and securing the family's inheritance. He is one of the most compelling Old Testament portraits of grace and foreshadows Christ the true Redeemer.

## Why They Matter
- **Theological significance:** Boaz demonstrates that true righteousness exceeds what the law requires. He does not merely follow gleaning laws — he pours out lavish provision, protection, and honor on a foreign widow who had no claim on him.
- **Role in redemptive history:** As the kinsman-redeemer who pays the price to restore what was lost and bring the outsider into the family, Boaz is a direct type of Christ, who redeems what humanity lost and brings the spiritually destitute into God's family.
- **Legacy:** Boaz's act of redemption produces Obed, whose line leads to David and ultimately to Jesus. His generosity that went beyond obligation is woven into the very genealogy of the Messiah.

## Character & Legacy
Boaz is a man whose character is visible in how he treats those beneath him. From the first moment, he sees Ruth not as a foreign interloper to be tolerated but as a woman of noble character worthy of honor. He speaks words of blessing over her before she has done anything for him. He protects her dignity among his workers. He feeds her at his table. All of this before any legal obligation has been established.

When the closer kinsman declines to redeem, Boaz steps forward without hesitation. His generosity is not a strategy — it is a reflection of a heart shaped by the hesed of God. In marrying Ruth, he does not condescend; he exalts her. He is, in the deepest sense, a picture of what it looks like when a redeemer acts not from duty alone but from love.

## New Testament Connections
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/40. Matthew/Matthew 1|Matthew 1:5]] — Boaz appears in the direct genealogy of Jesus, the ultimate Kinsman-Redeemer
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/58. Hebrews/Hebrews 9|Hebrews 9:12]] — Christ's redemption fulfills and surpasses what Boaz enacted: a permanent redemption by his own blood
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/42. Luke/Luke 4|Luke 4:18]] — Jesus announces he has come to proclaim freedom and restoration — the jubilee that Boaz's redemption foreshadowed
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/45. Romans/Romans 8|Romans 8:3]] — God sent his Son to do what no closer relative could do for us, redeeming fully what we could not redeem ourselves
