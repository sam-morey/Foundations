# Miriam

**Testament:** Old Testament
**Era:** The Exodus
**Appears In:** [[3. Overviews/02. Exodus Overview|Exodus]], [[3. Overviews/04. Numbers Overview|Numbers]]

## Who They Are
Miriam is the older sister of Moses and Aaron, a prophetess and worship leader in Israel who plays a crucial role in the deliverance narrative from the very beginning. She watches over the infant Moses in the Nile, leads the women of Israel in song and dance after the crossing of the Red Sea, and is explicitly named a prophetess — one of a very small number of women to receive that title in the Old Testament. Her later judgment for pride before God becomes one of the Torah's most sobering warnings about the cost of speaking against God's appointed leaders.

## Why They Matter
- **Theological significance:** Miriam's judgment illustrates that proximity to God's chosen leaders and gifts of prophecy do not exempt anyone from the requirement of humility and submission to God's appointed order. The severity of her leprosy — and Moses's immediate intercession for her — together reveal both the gravity of pride and the grace of forgiveness.
- **Role in redemptive history:** Miriam's song at the Red Sea (Exodus 15:21) is one of the oldest poems in the Bible and frames the Exodus as a victory to be celebrated and remembered. Her watch over the infant Moses secured the life of the one without whom the Exodus would not have occurred.
- **Legacy:** Micah 6:4 is remarkable: God explicitly names Miriam as one of three leaders he sent to Israel for the Exodus. She is not a footnote — she is part of the divine team deployed for Israel's redemption. Her song echoes in Mary's Magnificat and in the song of Moses in Revelation 15.

## Character & Legacy
Miriam is a woman of courage, initiative, and creative worship. Her quick thinking at the Nile — offering to find a Hebrew nurse for the baby, then fetching his own mother — saves Moses's first years and keeps his identity alive. Her leadership of the women in song after the Red Sea crossing is exuberant and specifically Spirit-empowered; she is the first person in Scripture to be called a "prophetess" in a narrative context.

Her failure in Numbers 12 is all the more striking for the stature she had earned. She and Aaron challenge Moses's unique authority, using his Cushite wife as a pretext while the real grievance seems to be Moses's singular access to God. God's response is immediate and severe: Miriam is struck with leprosy and shut outside the camp for seven days. The fact that Aaron escapes and Miriam bears the full consequence has been much discussed, but the episode's primary function is not to raise questions of fairness — it is to show that speaking against God's anointed is not merely a social offense but a spiritual one. Moses's intercession ("O God, please heal her") is as gracious a response as Scripture records.
