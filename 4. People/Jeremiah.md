## Who They Are
Jeremiah is the weeping prophet, called before his birth to speak God's word to Judah in the final decades before Babylon destroys Jerusalem. His ministry spans forty years of rejection, mockery, imprisonment, and isolation. He is not allowed to marry, not allowed to mourn or feast — his very life is a sign of coming desolation. Yet he contains the most personal prophetic lament in Scripture, and in the darkest hour of exile he announces the new covenant that will define the entire New Testament.

## Why They Matter
- **Theological significance:** Jeremiah confronts the deepest problem: the law cannot change the heart. The covenant the people keep breaking requires not better behavior but a new heart — and that is exactly what Jeremiah 31 promises. It is the most theologically significant covenant prophecy in the Old Testament.
- **Role in redemptive history:** The new covenant promise of Jeremiah 31:31–34 is the foundation text for the Lord's Supper ("the new covenant in my blood") and the entire argument of the book of Hebrews. Without Jeremiah's prophecy, the NT's covenant theology is incomplete.
- **Legacy:** Jeremiah is remembered as the prophet who suffered most visibly for his message, and who therefore foreshadows the suffering of Christ. He loved the people who rejected him and wept over the city that would kill its prophets.

## Character & Legacy
Jeremiah is the most emotionally exposed of all the prophets. His "confessions" — scattered through chapters 11–20 — are raw, unguarded, and sometimes shocking. He accuses God of deceiving him. He wishes he had never been born. He argues with God and then keeps preaching. This is not a man who has resolved his doubts; it is a man who keeps obeying in spite of them. His courage is not the absence of fear but faithfulness in the presence of it.

He purchases a field during the siege of Jerusalem — the city is about to be destroyed and he buys real estate — as an acted prophecy that God will restore what Babylon is taking. It is one of the great prophetic acts of faith in Scripture: defying appearances to declare what God has promised. He is a man who suffers not because he lacks faith but because he has it, and faithfulness in a faithless generation is the most costly posture of all.

## New Testament Connections
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/40. Matthew/Matthew 26|Matthew 26:28]] — Jesus at the Last Supper says "my blood of the covenant" — invoking Jeremiah 31's new covenant promise
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/58. Hebrews/Hebrews 8|Hebrews 8:8–12]] — Hebrews quotes Jeremiah 31 in full as the scriptural basis for the new and better covenant in Christ
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/58. Hebrews/Hebrews 10|Hebrews 10:16–17]] — Jeremiah 31:33–34 quoted again: "Their sins and lawless acts I will remember no more"
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/40. Matthew/Matthew 2|Matthew 2:17–18]] — Matthew quotes Jeremiah 31:15 (Rachel weeping) for the massacre of the innocents
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/44. Acts/Acts 2|Acts 2:38]] — Peter's call to receive the Spirit is the fulfillment of Jeremiah's promise of the law written on the heart
