# Martha

**Testament:** New Testament
**Era:** The Gospels
**Appears In:** [[3. Book Overviews/42. Luke Overview|Luke]], [[3. Book Overviews/43. John Overview|John]]

## Who She Is
Martha of Bethany is the practical, hospitable sister of Mary and Lazarus who welcomed Jesus into her home and served him faithfully. She is often remembered for her distracted busyness in Luke 10 — and Jesus's gentle correction is real — but she should be equally remembered for the great confession of faith she makes in John 11:27: "Yes, Lord; I believe that you are the Christ, the Son of God, who is coming into the world." Her story teaches that service and faith are not opposites, and that even when we come to Jesus with grief and questions, he meets us with himself.

## Why She Matters
- Her confession in John 11:27 — "I believe that you are the Christ, the Son of God" — rivals Peter's great confession in Matthew 16 and stands as one of the defining statements of NT faith
- Her story in Luke 10 is not simply a rebuke of busyness; it is an invitation to prioritize sitting with Jesus as the foundation from which all service flows
- As a woman who both served faithfully and confessed boldly, she models that faith is expressed through the whole person — hands and heart together

## Character & Legacy
Martha is a woman of action — she runs to Jesus when Lazarus dies, she speaks her grief and her faith plainly, she gets up and calls her sister. She does not simply accept the situation; she engages it. And Jesus meets her exactly there. Her practical question at the tomb ("Lord, by this time there will be an odor") is a very human moment of doubt right on the edge of a miracle — and it is not held against her. Jesus doesn't require perfect faith before he acts; he requires only that we come to him.

Martha teaches that genuine devotion to Jesus includes both the contemplative and the active. The rebuke in Luke 10 is not a dismissal of service but a reminder that service untethered from sitting with Jesus becomes anxious and resentful. Rooted in the one thing necessary, all her service becomes worship.
