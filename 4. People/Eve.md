## Who They Are
Eve is the first woman, created by God from Adam's rib as a suitable helper and companion. She is a co-image-bearer of God and the mother of all living, whose name Adam gives in faith despite the curse. She is the first to be deceived by the serpent, and her act of eating the forbidden fruit — followed by giving it to Adam — initiates the Fall of humanity.

## Why They Matter
- **Theological significance:** Eve's creation establishes the equal dignity and complementary design of men and women as both bearing the image of God. Her deception by the serpent reveals how sin enters through the distortion of God's word and a misplaced desire for autonomy.
- **Role in redemptive history:** Genesis 3:15 — the protoevangelium — is addressed in the context of Eve's seed. The coming Redeemer will be born of a woman, making Eve the mother not only of all the living but of the line that leads to Christ.
- **Legacy:** Eve is remembered as the first deceived, but also as the first to hear the promise of ultimate redemption. Her name itself — "mother of all living" — speaks hope in the shadow of death.

## Character & Legacy
Eve is introduced as the crown of creation, the one for whom all of creation was not quite complete without. She is relationally oriented, intellectually curious, and deeply relational with both God and her husband. Her failure is not stupidity but a subtle theological distortion: she is drawn away by a half-truth that flatters her desire for wisdom.

What is often overlooked is Eve's faith after the Fall. When she bears Cain she says, "I have gotten a man with the help of the Lord" — a recognition of divine partnership in the continuation of life. She later names Seth saying God has appointed another offspring in place of Abel, again pointing to her awareness of God's ongoing work. Her story is not only one of failure but of faith persisting amid the curse.
