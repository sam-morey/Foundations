# Nicodemus

**Testament:** New Testament
**Era:** The Gospels
**Appears In:** [[3. Overviews/43. John Overview|John]]

## Who He Is
Nicodemus is a Pharisee and member of the Jewish ruling council (the Sanhedrin) who came to Jesus by night — drawn to him but not yet willing to be seen. His nighttime visit becomes the occasion for Jesus's most famous words about new birth: "You must be born again" (John 3:7). He appears three times in John's Gospel, each time showing a deeper, if cautious, movement toward faith — until he finally steps into the open after the crucifixion to help bury Jesus.

## Why He Matters
- His conversation with Jesus in John 3 contains the most concentrated statement of the gospel in the NT, including John 3:16–17 — the passage perhaps more memorized than any other in Scripture
- His trajectory from secret seeker to public mourner at the cross is a picture of gradual, costly faith — the kind many religious people experience as they work through the implications of who Jesus is
- As a religious insider, he represents the truth that birth, education, and religious privilege cannot produce the new birth that only the Spirit gives

## Character & Legacy
Nicodemus is a picture of the honest religious seeker — someone who has everything the system can offer and yet senses that something is missing, that Jesus has something he lacks. He comes by night, which John's Gospel takes seriously as a symbol: he is not yet in the light. But he comes. And that first visit sets something in motion that doesn't stop.

By John 19, Nicodemus is no longer hiding. He brings a lavish, public quantity of burial spices and helps prepare the body of the man the authorities have just executed. This is no small step for a member of the Sanhedrin. Whatever the cost to his social standing and safety, he chooses to honor Jesus openly in death. John never tells us when exactly Nicodemus fully believed — but the trajectory is clear, and it ends at the foot of the cross.
