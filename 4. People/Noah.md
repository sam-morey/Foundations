# Noah

**Testament:** Old Testament
**Era:** Primeval History
**Appears In:** [[3. Book Overviews/01. Genesis Overview|Genesis]]

## Who They Are
Noah is a righteous man in a generation of pervasive wickedness, chosen by God to build an ark and preserve a remnant of humanity and creation through the judgment of the Flood. He stands as a new Adam figure — a fresh start for humanity after catastrophic judgment — and the recipient of the covenant of the rainbow. His obedience is remarkable; his later failure is a reminder that even the saved still need a greater rescuer.

## Why They Matter
- **Theological significance:** Noah's story demonstrates that God takes sin seriously enough to judge it globally, and that his grace is always present in the midst of judgment. The ark is salvation by grace through the provision of God — Noah did not design his own rescue.
- **Role in redemptive history:** The Noahic covenant (Genesis 9) establishes the stability of creation as the stage on which redemption will unfold. God's promise never again to destroy all life gives the world the continuity it needs for the coming of Christ.
- **Legacy:** Noah is remembered in Hebrews 11 as a man of faith who "condemned the world" by his righteous obedience. His story is the template Peter uses to explain baptism and salvation in 1 Peter 3.

## Character & Legacy
Noah is described simply but powerfully: "a righteous man, blameless in his generation; Noah walked with God." In a world saturated with violence and corruption, his life stood apart. His decades-long building project — an act of obedience with no visible confirmation until the rain came — is one of the most extraordinary examples of patient faith in Scripture.

Yet the Flood is not the end of the story. Almost immediately after leaving the ark, Noah gets drunk and lies exposed in his tent. The curse of sin did not wash away with the floodwaters. This episode is not incidental — it is the author's way of showing that a new beginning with the same fallen humanity will not ultimately solve the problem. Noah points beyond himself to one who will not merely survive the judgment but absorb it.
