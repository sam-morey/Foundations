## Who He Is
Jesus of Nazareth is the divine Son of God — fully God and fully human — who entered history as the fulfillment of every promise God made to Israel. He is the promised Messiah, the anointed King of David's line, who came not to establish an earthly kingdom by force but to lay down his life as a ransom for many. In him the entire story of Scripture reaches its climax: the covenant with Abraham, the law given through Moses, the kingdom promised to David, and the new covenant announced by the prophets all find their "yes" in Jesus (2 Corinthians 1:20). He is, as Paul declares, the one in whom "all things hold together" — the center of all creation, all history, and all redemption.

## Why He Matters
- As the fulfillment of every OT type and prophecy, Jesus is the one toward whom all of Scripture points — every sacrifice, every king, every prophet was a shadow of him
- As the true and better Moses, David, Priest, and Passover Lamb, he accomplishes what every human mediator and institution could only foreshadow
- As the one in whom all things hold together (Colossians 1:17), he is not merely a religious figure but the Lord of all creation and history
- As the risen Lord who sends the Spirit and commissions the church, his story does not end at the tomb — it breaks open into the new creation and the mission that continues to this day

## The Titles of Jesus
Jesus bears titles that together reveal the full breadth of who he is. **Son of God** declares his divine nature and unique relationship with the Father. **Son of Man** (drawn from Daniel 7) is his most frequent self-designation — the heavenly figure who receives an everlasting kingdom. **Messiah/Christ** ("anointed one") identifies him as the long-promised King of Israel. **Lord** (Kyrios) echoes the divine name and places him at the right hand of the Father. **Word (Logos)** — unique to John's Gospel — declares that Jesus is the eternal self-expression of God who became flesh. **Lamb of God** (John 1:29) ties him to the Passover and the sacrificial system. **Good Shepherd** (John 10) recalls Psalm 23 and Ezekiel 34, where God himself promises to shepherd his scattered people. **Bread of Life** and **Light of the World** (John 6, 8) claim the very sustenance and illumination that humanity most needs. And **the Way, the Truth, and the Life** (John 14:6) declares that there is no path to the Father except through him.

## The Cross and Resurrection
The cross is not a tragedy that God rescued from, but the very means by which God accomplished the rescue of the world. Jesus died as the substitute for sinners — bearing in his body the judgment that humanity deserved — a doctrine the New Testament calls substitutionary atonement (Isaiah 53; Romans 3:25; 2 Corinthians 5:21). But the cross is also a cosmic victory: in dying, Jesus defeated sin, death, and the powers of darkness (Colossians 2:15; Hebrews 2:14–15). The resurrection is the Father's vindication of the Son — his declaration that the sacrifice was accepted and death had no final claim. More than that, the resurrection is the firstfruits of the new creation (1 Corinthians 15:20–23): in Jesus rising bodily from the dead, the age to come has broken into the present age, and all who are united to him by faith share in that resurrection life now and fully on the last day.

## Old Testament Roots
- **Adam** — Jesus is the Last Adam (Romans 5; 1 Corinthians 15), the obedient human who succeeds where Adam failed and restores what was lost
- **Abraham** — In Jesus, all the nations of the earth are blessed (Galatians 3:16); he is the singular "seed" in whom the Abrahamic covenant is fulfilled
- **Moses** — Jesus is the true Lawgiver and Mediator of a better covenant (Hebrews 3; Matthew 5); he delivers his people not from Egypt but from sin and death
- **David** — Jesus is the Son of David, the eternal King whose throne will never end (2 Samuel 7; Luke 1:32–33; Revelation 22:16)
- **The Temple** — Jesus is the true dwelling place of God among his people (John 2:19–21); in him the presence of God takes on flesh
- **The Passover Lamb** — Jesus is sacrificed at Passover; his blood marks those who are spared from judgment (1 Corinthians 5:7; John 19:14)
- **The Suffering Servant** — Isaiah 53 describes one who is "pierced for our transgressions" and "crushed for our iniquities" — a portrait Jesus fulfills at Calvary
- **The High Priest** — Jesus is the great High Priest who offers himself as the once-for-all sacrifice, entering the true heavenly sanctuary (Hebrews 4:14–10:18)
