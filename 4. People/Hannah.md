# Hannah

**Testament:** Old Testament
**Era:** The Judges
**Appears In:** [[3. Book Overviews/09. 1 Samuel Overview|1 Samuel]]

## Who They Are
Hannah is the mother of Samuel, the last judge and first great prophet of Israel. A barren woman in a culture that measured worth by childbearing, she brings her anguish to God in raw, honest prayer at the tabernacle in Shiloh. God answers her prayer, she dedicates the child back to God, and her song of praise becomes one of the great theological poems of the Old Testament — the direct model for Mary's Magnificat.

## Why They Matter
- **Theological significance:** Hannah models what genuine, desperate prayer looks like — honest, persistent, and submitted to God's will. She does not pretend to be fine; she pours out her soul. Her prayer is answered not because it was polished but because it was real.
- **Role in redemptive history:** Without Hannah's vow and her willingness to give Samuel back, there is no Samuel — and without Samuel, there is no anointing of David. She is a quiet hinge on which Israel's monarchic history turns.
- **Legacy:** Hannah's song in 1 Samuel 2 is one of the most theologically rich passages in the Old Testament. It announces the reversal of human power structures under God's sovereignty in language that Mary will echo centuries later at the announcement of the Messiah.

## Character & Legacy
Hannah is defined by the combination of deep suffering and deep trust. She is provoked cruelly by her rival Peninnah and weeps without eating. Yet she does not become bitter toward God — she goes to him. Her prayer at Shiloh is so fervent that it looks like drunkenness. She is misread by the very priest she came to meet. And yet she absorbs his correction graciously, receives his blessing, and leaves with her face no longer downcast — before anything had physically changed.

When Samuel is born and then given back, Hannah demonstrates extraordinary faith. She kept her vow even though it cost her the child she had prayed for. The God who opened her womb is the God who owns the child — and she trusts him with both. In return, God fills her house with five more children. Her story is a sustained argument that honest, submitted prayer is never wasted.

## New Testament Connections
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/42. Luke/Luke 1|Luke 1:46–55]] — Mary's Magnificat is modeled closely on Hannah's song; both celebrate God reversing fortune and exalting the humble
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/42. Luke/Luke 1|Luke 1:13]] — Zechariah's barrenness story echoes Hannah's; God opens what was closed for the sake of redemptive history
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/58. Hebrews/Hebrews 11|Hebrews 11:11]] — Hannah's faith that God could do the impossible parallels the faith commended throughout Hebrews 11
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/59. James/James 5|James 5:16]] — Hannah is a premier example of the fervent, earnest prayer of a righteous person that "avails much"
