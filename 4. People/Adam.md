## Who They Are
Adam is the first human being, created by God from the dust of the ground and given the breath of life. He is the federal head and representative of all humanity, placed in the Garden of Eden to work it and keep it. His act of disobedience against God's command plunged all of creation into sin and death, making him the pivotal figure at the origin of the human story.

## Why They Matter
- **Theological significance:** Adam's creation in the image of God establishes the dignity and value of every human being. His fall reveals the catastrophic consequences of choosing self over God and the reality that sin distorts the image we bear.
- **Role in redemptive history:** As the federal head of humanity, Adam's sin brought condemnation to all. God's response in Genesis 3:15 — the first gospel promise (the protoevangelium) — sets the entire redemptive story in motion.
- **Legacy:** Adam is remembered as both the origin of human dignity and the origin of human ruin. He stands as the backdrop against which Christ, the Last Adam, is fully understood.

## Character & Legacy
Adam is given every imaginable gift — life, relationship with God, a perfect companion, meaningful work, and a beautiful world — and yet he fails at the one point of trust. When tempted, he is present with Eve but silent, abdicating his responsibility. He then shifts blame to Eve and indirectly to God ("the woman you gave me"). This pattern of silence, passivity, and deflection marks the first human failure of leadership and love.

Yet Adam is also the recipient of God's first act of grace after the Fall: God makes garments of skin to cover their shame. Even in judgment, mercy is woven in. The promise of Genesis 3:15 means that even Adam's catastrophic failure is not the final word. He names his wife "Eve — the mother of all living," an act of faith that life will continue despite the curse.
