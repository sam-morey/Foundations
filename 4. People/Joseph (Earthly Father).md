## Who He Is
Joseph of Nazareth is the carpenter of David's line who became the earthly father and protector of Jesus — a man the Gospels describe as "righteous" who never speaks a single recorded word. He appears in moments of crisis and obeys without complaint: when told Mary's child is from the Holy Spirit, when warned to flee to Egypt, when called back to Israel. His silence in the narrative is not absence but a picture of quiet, steadfast faithfulness.

## Why He Matters
- Joseph demonstrates that faithfulness to God often looks like obedience in obscurity — doing the right thing without recognition, record, or fanfare
- His legal descent from David (Matthew 1:1–17) is the mechanism by which Jesus is legally the "Son of David" — fulfilling the Davidic covenant through Joseph's adoption of Jesus
- His trust in God when everything seemed dishonored — when Mary's pregnancy could have led to public shame — shows a man whose righteousness was real, not performative

## Character & Legacy
Joseph is the silent guardian of the incarnation. He is never quoted, yet every time he appears he obeys immediately and completely. Matthew's account of Joseph is structured around angelic dreams, and each time Joseph wakes and does exactly what he is told — a pattern that recalls the other Joseph of Genesis (also a dreamer, also sent to Egypt, also a preserver of God's people).

He disappears from the Gospel narrative before Jesus's public ministry begins, likely having died sometime in Jesus's early adulthood. But his legacy is woven into Jesus's own formation: a carpenter's son raised in a home of quiet faith, Davidic heritage, and obedient trust in God.
