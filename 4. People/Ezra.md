# Ezra

**Testament:** Old Testament
**Era:** The Return
**Appears In:** [[3. Overviews/15. Ezra Overview|Ezra]], [[3. Overviews/16. Nehemiah Overview|Nehemiah]]

## Who They Are
Ezra is a scribe and priest who leads a second wave of returning exiles from Babylon to Jerusalem around 458 BC, nearly eighty years after the first return under Zerubbabel. He is described as skilled in the Law of Moses, devoted to studying it, doing it, and teaching it. His public reading of the Law in Nehemiah 8 — which brings the entire community to tears and then to celebration — stands as one of the most dramatic scenes of Scripture-centered renewal in the Bible.

## Why They Matter
- **Theological significance:** Ezra demonstrates that spiritual renewal is inseparable from the Word of God. The community's transformation in Nehemiah 8 flows directly from the public reading and explanation of Scripture — not from programs or political restoration but from hearing and understanding God's word.
- **Role in redemptive history:** Ezra's work to reconstitute the covenant community around the Law is the religious foundation without which the later New Testament community — which began as a reform movement within Judaism — could not have existed. He preserves and transmits the scriptural tradition.
- **Legacy:** Jewish tradition credits Ezra with establishing the canon, founding the Great Synagogue, and shaping the reading of Torah that becomes central to Judaism and eventually to the early church. He is the father of the scribal tradition and of Scripture-centered community life.

## Character & Legacy
Ezra's defining characteristic is stated plainly in Ezra 7:10: he set his heart to study, to do, and to teach. The order matters. He is not a theorist; he lives the Law before he teaches it, and he teaches only what he has practiced. This integrity gives his teaching its weight.

His prayer of confession in Ezra 9 is striking in its identification. He has not personally participated in the intermarriages he is lamenting, yet he tears his robe, pulls out his hair, and kneels in prostrate confession — "I am too ashamed and disgraced to lift my face to you." He feels the sin of his people as his own. This is intercession that carries the weight of belonging to a community, the same posture Daniel takes in Daniel 9. It is a portrait of priestly solidarity that foreshadows the One who would take on the sin of others fully.

## New Testament Connections
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/40. Matthew/Matthew 13|Matthew 13:52]] — Jesus's "scribe trained for the kingdom" who brings out new and old treasure echoes Ezra's model of the Scripture-shaped teacher
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/44. Acts/Acts 13|Acts 13:5]] — The synagogue reading of Scripture that defines Paul's missionary approach grew directly from the Ezra tradition of public Torah reading
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/44. Acts/Acts 15|Acts 15:21]] — Moses (Torah) is read in synagogues every Sabbath — a pattern Ezra established and the early church inherited
- [[2. The Bible (ESV)/58. Hebrews/Hebrews 4|Hebrews 4:12]] — The living and active word that penetrates and judges is the same word Ezra devoted his life to reading, doing, and teaching
